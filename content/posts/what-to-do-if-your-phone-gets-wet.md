<h2>What to do if your phone gets wet in the first minute</h2>
<p>Water in a phone needs quick action, and early removal lowers the risk of deeper soaking. Act fast, stay calm. Lift the phone out right away. How fast water gets in depends on the phone design, the state of its seals and the kind of liquid involved. A case can trap water against the body, so take it off. <strong>Speed matters</strong> more than any drying trick you try later.</p>
<p>Your next move depends on how wet it got. A splash needs a wipe. Patience does the rest. A full dip is different. Switching the phone off cuts the risk of a short circuit while parts are wet. How-To Geek gives that advice for phones without strong water resistance.</p>
<p>Speed helps, and rough handling hurts. Do not shake the phone hard, because hard shakes drive water past the seals around buttons and trays. Hard shaking can push water further inside. Pressing buttons again and again can also force water past seals. Wipe the outside with a soft cloth, then set the phone down and leave it alone for a good while. <strong>Calm handling</strong> protects the seals that keep water out.</p>
<p>Drips from a wet sleeve can undo good drying work and send fresh water straight back into a drying port. Keep the phone away from sinks and wet towels. A dry table in a warm room suits it better. Use your eyes only. Never use a tool.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Lift the phone out of the water right away.</li><li>Take the case off.</li><li>Switch the phone off if it went fully under water, because power and water make a risky pair.</li><li>Tap it gently against your hand with the port facing down.</li><li>Wipe the outside with a soft cloth.</li><li>Set it on a dry table and keep the charger unplugged.</li></ol>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Do not plug in a charger while the port is wet. Power plus water can corrode the pins inside.</div>

<h2>Why water hurts the charging port first</h2>
<p>Most damage starts at the port. That port is the connector, the small slot where your cable plugs in. Water sits in that slot easily and clings to the walls long after the outside looks dry. It also sits around the speaker holes. Those openings give water a path inside the body.</p>
<p>Inside the port, tiny metal pins carry power and data, and each pin sits close to its neighbour in tight rows. Water on live pins can start <strong>corrosion</strong>, a slow eating away of metal that weakens contact a little more each day. Corroded pins charge poorly or stop working. Apple warns that charging a wet connector can cause permanent damage. That warning shapes every step below.</p>
<p>Modern phones carry an IP rating, a code that grades how well a sealed body resists dust and water in lab tests. Many recent phones resist splashes and brief dips in lab tests. Check the official spec sheet for your exact model before trusting any rating. Resistance fades as seals age, and a single drop or a screen repair can weaken it a lot. Liquid damage also falls outside the standard warranty, per Apple. Treat the rating as <strong>backup protection</strong>, not permission to swim.</p>
<p>Seals around buttons and trays age with heat and knocks. A cracked screen also breaks the water barrier. Phones fare differently in the same spill. Judge by condition. Ignore the box.</p>
<h2>How to dry the phone the right way</h2>
<p>Apple gives a clear drying routine for a wet iPhone, and every step in it protects the port first. Tap the phone gently against your hand. Keep the connector facing down so liquid runs out. That tap clears loose water. It does not clear water deeper inside, so drying still takes time.</p>
<p>Leave it on a dry table. Time does the work. A table near a fan works well because moving air pulls moisture out of every small opening. Apple support advises waiting <strong>tens of minutes</strong> before any cable test. A returning liquid alert means a longer wait. Full drying can take <strong>up to a day</strong>, per Apple support.</p>
<p>Patience pays off when the phone looks dry but will not charge. A simple charger reset helps here. Unplug the cable from the adapter. Unplug the adapter from the wall. Plug both back in and try again. That reset clears a confused charger without forcing power through a port that may still hold a little water.</p>
<p>Airflow helps more than sealed boxes or bags, because still air inside a box turns damp and stays damp. Moving air carries moisture away from openings. A small fan across the table is enough. Keep the phone port facing down or sideways so water can drain.</p>
<h2>Myths you can ignore about rice and heat</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th?q=bowl+of+rice+beside+phone&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="bowl of rice beside phone" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Rice is famous. Apple support advises against it. Apple warns that rice dust can damage the phone. Small grains and powder can lodge in the port. Rice also dries the air around the phone very slowly. Skip the rice. Plain <strong>dry air</strong> does the same job without the dust.</p>
<p>Heat feels fast and causes its own harm, because hot air moves water around instead of removing it from the phone. Apple says not to dry an iPhone with an outside heat source. Maker guidance warns against heat sources, since blown air can move water inward and heat can stress parts inside. Hot air can also harm the battery and screen, and it can soften the glue that holds seals in place. <strong>Compressed air</strong> carries the same risk of forcing water inward.</p>
<p>Cotton swabs and paper towels look harmless in a port. Maker guidance says to keep foreign objects out of the connector. A swab can leave fibres behind and bend delicate pins. A paper towel corner can tear and stay stuck. Wipe the outside only. Air handles the inside.</p>
<p>Dust clogs speakers too. That clog muffles sound long after drying ends. Heat guns and ovens add fire risk to the list. Slow air drying avoids every one of those problems.</p>
<div class="note warn"><b>Common mistake:</b> Rice looks helpful and does harm. Dust and grains can lodge in the port and speaker.</div>
<h2>When you can charge again and when to wait</h2>
<p>Charging too soon is the costliest mistake here, because one wet charge can undo a whole day of careful drying. Power through a wet port can corrode the pins. Patience wins here. That damage can be permanent. iPhone XS, iPhone XR and newer models show a liquid-detected alert. The alert tells you to unplug and wait. <strong>Wait it out</strong> every time it appears.</p>
<p>Many Android phones guard the port in a similar way. A moisture-detected warning blocks USB charging until the port dries, and it keeps blocking while any water remains inside. The wording differs by maker, but the logic matches. Dry first, charge second. If the warning returns, the port still holds water. Give it more drying time.</p>
<p>Wireless charging skips the port. A wireless charger keeps the wet port out of it, once the outside is dry. Wipe the back dry before you set the phone down. Heat from charging still argues for patience, because a warm phone can draw damp air deeper as it cools. When in doubt, wait longer than feels needed. <strong>Extra hours</strong> cost less than a dead port.</p>
<p>Alerts that return after a cable test mean water remains, and the port needs a fresh stretch of drying time. Unplug at once and restart the drying wait. Repeated cable tests add risk without adding information. One test after a long wait tells you enough.</p>
<h2>Signs the water did real damage</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th?q=phone+with+fogged+camera+lens&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="phone with fogged camera lens" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Some water leaves worse traces than other water, and the kind of liquid matters as much as the amount. Salt water speeds up corrosion on metal parts. Sugary drinks leave sticky residue as they dry. Fresh water is kinder. It still needs full drying. A sea dip or a spilled soda deserves extra patience.</p>
<p>Salt and sugar explain it. Residue can keep pins from making clean contact. Corrosion can grow after the phone feels dry. Charging that comes and goes hints at port trouble. A phone that will not charge after full drying needs a repair check.</p>
<p>Muffled sound is common and often temporary. Water in the speaker blocks sound until it evaporates. Give the speaker a full day of drying time before you judge it broken or pay for a repair. Watch for heat. Screen glitches, random restarts and a hot body point the other way. Those signs suggest water reached deeper parts. <strong>Book a repair</strong> rather than forcing more charges.</p>
<p>Residue from salt or sugar can hide inside the port, out of sight, and keep blocking charge for weeks. That hidden layer blocks clean contact with the cable. A repair shop can clean and inspect the port safely. <strong>Professional cleaning</strong> beats poking at pins at home.</p>
<ul><li>Port warning: wait longer before any cable goes in, even for a quick test.</li><li>Speaker muffle: wait a day.</li><li>Charging failure: try the adapter reset Apple describes.</li><li>Visible damage: book a repair check instead of forcing charge.</li></ul>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Can I use my phone while it dries?</h3>
<p>Light use is usually fine after a splash. Keep the charger unplugged. Avoid pressing buttons again and again while water sits inside.</p>
<h3>How long should I wait before charging?</h3>
<p>Give it tens of minutes before any cable test, per Apple support. A returning alert can mean waiting up to a day. Trust the alert over the clock.</p>
<h3>My speaker sounds muffled. Is it broken?</h3>
<p>Water in the speaker often muffles sound for a while. That muffle usually clears as the water dries. Judge the speaker only after the phone has had full drying time.</p>
<h3>Does wireless charging work with a wet phone?</h3>
<p>A wireless charger avoids the wet port, once the outside is dry. Wipe the back dry first. When in doubt, extra drying time is the safer choice, because waiting costs hours and a corroded port costs far more.</p>
