<h2>What the cache is and what clearing it removes</h2>
<p>Every app keeps a stash of temporary files, called its cache, so it can open faster. A photo app saves small copies of pictures you already viewed. A news app saves parts of pages you already read. Your phone reads those copies instead of fetching them again, so screens load in a blink.</p>
<p>Those files build up over weeks. A video app that streams daily can hold hundreds of megabytes of thumbnails. A browser keeps images from every site you visit. It is not junk, though. It does a real job, and deleting it only makes the app fetch the same files again next time.</p>
<p>Clearing the cache removes those copies and nothing else. Your account stays signed in. Your chats, photos, downloads and settings stay where they are. The app may feel slow for a minute while it rebuilds the files it needs, then it runs as before. You trade a little space and a fresh start for one slow reload.</p>
<p>Cache is not the same as storage. Storage is everything the app has saved on your phone, and the cache is one small part of it. Both buttons sit side by side in the same menu. Keep them straight and the rest is easy.</p>

<h2>How to clear cache on Android, app by app</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th?q=Android+Storage+and+cache+screen+Clear+cache&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="Android Storage and cache screen Clear cache" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Android has no button that clears every app&#x27;s cache at once. Google removed that switch with Android 8, so each app keeps its own cache and each one is cleared on its own screen. That sounds slow, but one app is usually the whole problem. Start with the app that crashes, shows old content, or eats the most storage.</p>
<p>On nearly every Android phone, the path stays the same. Open Settings and tap Apps to see the list of apps on your phone. Tap See all apps if the list looks short. Pick the app, then tap Storage and cache. Samsung phones label that screen Storage. The button you want is Clear cache. Menu names shift a little between Android versions and brands, so look for the same words in a different order if a label differs.</p>
<p>Skip Settings with a faster route. Press and hold the app icon on your home screen or in the app drawer. Then tap the small i in a circle, labelled App info. That opens the same app page, and the storage screen is one tap away. Use it when you know which app needs the clean. The result is identical either way.</p>
<p>Sort the list when you are hunting for space. In Settings, tap Apps, then open the menu in the corner and choose Sort by size. Biggest apps land at the top, and the cache figure next to each one shows where the bulk sits. Clear the cache of the top two or three and check the storage number again. Video, social and shopping apps usually lead the list. Open Settings, then Storage first to see what the phone holds before you start.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Open Settings and tap Apps.</li><li>Tap See all apps, then tap the app that misbehaves or takes the most space.</li><li>Tap Storage and cache. Samsung phones call this screen Storage.</li><li>Tap Clear cache and wait a second. The cache figure drops to zero or close to it.</li></ol>
<h2>How to clear Chrome&#x27;s cache on Android</h2>
<p>Chrome keeps its own cache, separate from the cache you clear in Settings. Clearing an app&#x27;s cache in Settings does touch Chrome, but the browser&#x27;s own menu gives finer control. Use it when pages load old versions, images fail to show, or a site looks broken. The fix takes under a minute.</p>
<p>Open Chrome and tap the three dots in the top right corner. In Settings, choose Privacy and security, then Delete browsing data. Tick Cached images and files, and untick Browsing history and Cookies and site data unless you want those gone too. Choose Delete data and confirm. Chrome keeps you signed in to Google sites, since clearing the cache alone does not sign you out.</p>
<p>Cookies are different from cache. A cookie holds your identity on a site, and cache holds copies of page parts like images. Clearing cookies signs you out of those sites. Tick only Cached images and files when all you want is a fresh copy of a page. Leave the other boxes alone and your logins survive.</p>
<p>Other browsers work the same way. Samsung Internet hides its switch under the menu button, then Settings, then Personal browsing data, then Delete browsing data. Firefox has its own clear-data screen in its settings. That rule never changes: pick cached images and files only, and leave history and cookies ticked off unless you mean to lose them.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Tick only Cached images and files. The other boxes sign you out of sites.</div>
<h2>Clear cache vs clear storage: the button you must not mix up</h2>
<p>Two buttons share the same screen, and only one of them is safe to tap without thinking. Clear cache deletes temporary files and leaves the rest alone. Clear storage, called Clear data on Samsung phones, wipes the app back to the day you installed it. Names sound close, and the buttons sit a finger apart. Tapping the wrong one costs you your logins, and that is why the two are separated below.</p>
<p>Clear storage logs you out of the app and deletes what the app saved on the phone. Downloaded files inside the app, offline playlists, saved game progress and custom settings all go. Photos the app downloaded for offline viewing go too, unless they live in your gallery. Anything the app synced to its own cloud account comes back when you sign in again. Anything it never synced is gone for good. Back up first if the app holds anything you would miss.</p>
<p>Treat Clear storage as a last resort, not a first step. When an app misbehaves, clear its cache first. Only when that fails should you tap Clear storage, and only after you know what the app will lose.</p>

<h2>When clearing the cache helps, and when it does nothing</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th?q=phone+storage+screen+showing+cached+data+size&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="phone storage screen showing cached data size" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>One tap fixes a narrow set of problems. It helps when an app shows old pictures, a page loads with missing images, or an app crashes right after an update. A stale copy of a file is the cause, and a fresh copy is the cure. Those are the cases where one tap earns its keep.</p>
<p>Healthy phones gain no speed from a cache clear. A cache exists to make apps faster, so wiping it forces every app to fetch its files again. Your phone may feel slower for a day, not faster. Cache-clearing apps promise a faster phone. They sell the opposite of what the cache does.</p>
<p>Space comes back only briefly. Cache refills as you use your apps, and heavy apps rebuild it fast with daily use. If storage is the real problem, look at videos, downloads and large apps instead. Clearing the cache buys a little room for a little while, and the phone takes it back.</p>
<p>A few faults sit beyond the cache. A cache clear does not remove a virus, fix a weak battery, or undo a bad update. Those need their own fixes: a malware scan, a battery check, or an update from the maker. Treat the cache as one tool in a small kit, not a cure-all. When the problem survives a cache clear, the cause sits somewhere else. A second clear will not find it.</p>
<h2>What to do when clearing the cache does not fix it</h2>
<p>Some problems outlive a cache clear, and the next steps are cheap. Restart the phone first. A restart closes stuck background work and frees memory. It takes less than a minute. Open the app again and see if the fault is gone before you try anything harder.</p>
<p>Update the app next. Open the Play Store, search for the app, and tap Update if the button shows one. App updates fix bugs that no amount of clearing can reach. A crash that started after an update often ends with the next one. Turn on auto-updates for the app if you keep forgetting this step.</p>
<p>Stop the app, then clear its cache a second time. Open the app page in Settings, tap Force stop, then open Storage and cache and tap Clear cache. A running app can write fresh cache files as you work, so stopping it first makes the clear stick. This is the step most guides skip, and it is the one that makes the difference when a clear seems to do nothing.</p>
<p>When nothing helps, the problem may sit outside the app. Check how much free storage the phone has in Settings, then Storage. A phone with almost no free space behaves badly. No cache clear can fix that, and the cure is deleting videos or moving photos to the cloud. If the app still fails on a phone with room to spare, uninstall it and install it fresh. That resets the app completely. It is the honest end of the road for a fix you can do yourself.</p>
<p>Greyed-out Clear cache buttons are normal on some apps. System apps and a few carrier apps lock their cache, since clearing it can break core functions. When the button is greyed out, leave it alone and move on. The phone manages those caches itself, and the button stays locked for a reason.</p>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Does clearing the cache delete my photos or messages?</h3>
<p>No. Photos, messages, chats and downloads live in the app&#x27;s storage, not its cache. Clearing the cache removes temporary copies only. Your files stay exactly where they were.</p>
<h3>Will clearing the cache sign me out of my apps?</h3>
<p>Clearing the cache keeps you signed in. Only Clear storage logs you out, because it wipes the app&#x27;s saved data. If a site asks you to sign in again, you cleared cookies in the browser, not the cache.</p>
<h3>How often should I clear the cache on Android?</h3>
<p>Only when an app misbehaves or shows old content. There is no schedule to keep, and clearing it weekly gains nothing. Android clears cached files on its own when storage runs low.</p>
<h3>Can I clear the cache of all apps at once?</h3>
<p>No single switch does that. Android dropped the clear-all option with Android 8. Samsung&#x27;s Device care can tidy up junk across apps, but it does not clear every cache. Work through the apps one at a time, starting with the biggest.</p>
