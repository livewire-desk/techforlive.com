<h2>What Google Gemini Tasks is, in plain words</h2>
<p>Gemini Tasks is Google&#x27;s AI agent, the part of the Gemini app that does jobs instead of answering questions. You give it a task in plain words, and it works through the steps on its own. It can plan a week of meals, sort a crowded inbox, or ring a shop to ask about stock. A normal Gemini chat stops after it answers. Tasks keeps going until the job is done, then reports back.</p>
<p>Both the name and the price are new. Google showed a first version of this agent earlier in the year, and paying subscribers tried it first. Google renamed it Tasks and opened it to free users. Google&#x27;s Josh Woodward, vice president for Labs, the Gemini app and AI Studio, said the free rollout covers hundreds of millions of people.</p>
<p>Strip it down and the idea is simple. A chat waits for your next question. Tasks takes a goal and works out the steps, checks your calendar, reads your mail, and acts. That is the shift Google is selling here. Tasks sits inside the Gemini app, next to Chat, as a toggle above the prompt box. Your old Spark tasks and schedules stay put, and they now sit in the sidebar under the new name.</p>

<h2>Who gets Gemini Tasks free, and who still pays</h2>
<p>Free covers six countries. The Verge and Google&#x27;s own announcement name the US, Canada, Japan, South Korea, Australia and India. If you live in one of those, and you meet three conditions, Tasks costs nothing. You must be 18 or over, signed in with a personal Google account, and have Gemini History switched on. A work or school account does not count.</p>
<p>Outside those six countries, Tasks still needs a paid plan. Google AI Pro or Ultra opens it, and the price depends on where you live. Four places get nothing at all for now. The European Economic Area, the United Kingdom, Switzerland and Nigeria are left out, per Google&#x27;s help page. Google has said nothing about when, or if, those places join the free rollout.</p>
<p>Google draws the line by country, not by phone. A US traveller abroad may still see Tasks, while a UK reader at home will not. That sounds odd, and it is the part readers trip over most. Check the country on your Google account before you hunt for the toggle. If your account sits in one of the six, Tasks should appear as the Gemini app updates on your phone.</p>
<h2>What Tasks can do that a chat cannot</h2>

<p>Tasks does the follow-through. Ask it to plan dinners for the week. It can check what is in season, build a shopping list, and put the shop run on your calendar. Ask it to clear your inbox, and it can sort mail into piles and draft replies for you to approve. The point is the doing. You state the goal once, and the agent handles the small steps in between.</p>
<p>Some jobs reach into the real world. In the US, Tasks can ring a business to compare prices, check stock, or book an appointment. It says it is an AI before it speaks. Those tricks are US-only for now, and the calling feature does not work in every state. Texting the agent needs a US phone number on a supported carrier.</p>
<p>Most people will use the everyday jobs first. It can watch for a price drop, remind you about a bill, or pull the numbers out of a statement and explain them. It can also run the same job on a schedule, like a weekly meal plan every Sunday. Each job sits in the sidebar, so you can check what it did, change it, or stop it. Nothing runs in secret.</p>
<h2>How to switch Tasks on and run your first task</h2>
<p>Start in the Gemini app, on the web or on your phone. Look just above the prompt box, where you type. You will see two words: Chat and Task. Tap Task. That one switch changes how the app works. If you do not see the toggle, check that your app is up to date and that History is on in Gemini settings.</p>
<p>Pick a small first job, not a big one. A good test is a meal plan for three days, or a tidy-up of one inbox folder. Watch what it does, and check each step before you let it act on its own. The first run shows you how much freedom you are comfortable giving it. Once you trust it with small jobs, give it bigger ones.</p>
<p>Give it one clear instruction, not three at once. Say what you want, when you want it, and what good looks like. A vague ask gets a vague result, and a precise ask gets a precise one. If the result misses, say so in plain words and let it try again. The agent learns from the correction inside that job.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Open the Gemini app and sign in with your personal Google account.</li><li>Tap the Task toggle above the prompt box.</li><li>Type one job in plain words, with a deadline.</li><li>Check the plan it shows you before it acts.</li><li>Open the job list to change a job or stop it.</li></ol>
<h2>The free limits you will run into</h2>
<p>There is still a ceiling on free use. Google gives free accounts a standard usage limit. Heavy days will hit the wall sooner than light ones. When the budget runs out, Tasks pauses until it resets. That is the trade for not paying.</p>
<p>Two more caps shape what you can set up. Free accounts have caps on how many schedules they can keep and how many tasks can run at once. A schedule is a job that repeats, like a Sunday meal plan. A task is a one-off job, like comparing prices on a washing machine. Count them before you build a long list.</p>
<p>Paid plans lift those caps, with more room for repeating jobs and heavier use. Whether that is worth paying for depends on how many repeating jobs you run. Most readers will not need it. Try the free tier for a week first, and count how often you hit a limit before you decide.</p>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Free Tasks pauses when its budget runs out. A job with a deadline should start early, not at the last hour.</div>
<h2>What to check before Tasks touches your inbox</h2>
<p>An agent is only useful if it can reach your stuff. Tasks needs your calendar to book things, your inbox to sort mail, and your files to find numbers. That access is the whole point, and it is also the risk. Read the permission screen before you tap yes. It lists each account and each kind of data the agent can touch.</p>
<p>Keep the agent on a short leash at first. Give it one account, not all of them, and one kind of job. Look over what it did after each job, before you give it the next one. If it booked something wrong, cancel it straight away and tell the agent what went wrong. Starting small is not caution for its own sake. It is how you learn what the agent does with access.</p>
<p>Money jobs need one more check. Set a rule for yourself before you let the agent near a payment. Decide what it may spend, and what it must ask about first. A price cap in your head is not a cap in the app. Until you know where the line sits, keep purchases as a job you approve each time.</p>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Is Gemini Tasks the same as Spark?</h3>
<p>Yes. Google renamed Spark to Tasks in November 2026, and the agent works the same way. Your old Spark tasks and schedules moved over with the new name. The big change is the price: it is now free in six countries instead of paid-only.</p>
<h3>Is Tasks really free, or is there a catch?</h3>
<p>It is free in the US, Canada, Japan, South Korea, Australia and India, with no subscription needed. The catch is the limits: a usage budget, caps on schedules, and caps on tasks running at once. Outside those six countries you still need a paid Google AI plan.</p>
<h3>Can I use Tasks in the UK or Europe?</h3>
<p>Not yet. If you live outside the six free countries, the toggle will not appear in your Gemini app. Google has not said when the rollout widens.</p>
<h3>What happens to my old Spark tasks?</h3>
<p>They carry over. Google says existing Spark tasks and schedules sit in the Gemini sidebar under the new Tasks name. You do not need to rebuild them. Check the sidebar after the update to make sure yours are all there.</p>
<h3>Does Tasks read my emails and calendar?</h3>
<p>It reads only what the job needs, and only from accounts you connect. Check the permission screen before you agree. You can change or remove access in Gemini settings at any time.</p>
