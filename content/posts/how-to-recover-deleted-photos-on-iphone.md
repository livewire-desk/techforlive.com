<h2>How to recover deleted photos on iPhone from Recently Deleted</h2>
<p>Deleting a photo does not erase it at once. That photo moves to an album called Recently Deleted, where it stays for <strong>30 days</strong> before Apple deletes it for good. Its countdown starts the day you delete the photo, and each photo shows how many days it has left. Check this album first, before you try anything else. Act fast.</p>
<p>Start in the Photos app on your iPhone. Swipe up past your photo grid until you see Utilities. On iOS 17 and earlier, tap Albums at the bottom instead, then scroll to Utilities. Choose Recently Deleted in that list. Tap View Album, then use Face ID or Touch ID to open it, or your passcode if Face ID fails. Inside, deleted photos and videos appear newest first. Each item shows its remaining days.</p>
<p>Tap one photo to see it full screen, then tap Recover at the bottom and confirm. The photo lands back in your library on its original date. It leaves Recently Deleted the moment you confirm.</p>
<p>You can also bring back several photos at once. Tap Select at the top, choose each photo you want, then tap the More button and choose Recover. The same menu holds Recover All, which brings back the whole album in one tap. Confirm once, and every photo returns, which beats tapping through hundreds of holiday snaps one by one.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Open Photos and swipe up to Utilities (on iOS 17 and earlier, tap Albums at the bottom).</li><li>Tap Recently Deleted, then tap View Album.</li><li>Use Face ID or Touch ID to open the album.</li><li>Tap a photo, then tap Recover and confirm.</li><li>For several photos, tap Select, choose each one, tap More, then tap Recover.</li></ol>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Tapping Delete inside Recently Deleted erases the photo at once. The 30-day wait ends the moment you confirm.</div>

<h2>Recover deleted photos on iCloud.com or a Mac</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th?q=iCloud+Photos+Recently+Deleted+album+in+web+browser&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="iCloud Photos Recently Deleted album in web browser" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Your Recently Deleted album also lives on the web. Sign in at iCloud.com with your Apple Account, then click Photos and choose Recently Deleted in the sidebar. That same 30-day countdown applies here. Pick a photo and click Recover, and it shows up in your library on every device that uses iCloud Photos. This route helps when your phone is lost, broken, or out of reach.</p>
<p>A Mac works the same way. Open Photos on a Mac, then find Recently Deleted in the sidebar, grouped with the other library collections. Use Touch ID or your login password to open the album. Click the photo you want, then click Recover. It appears in your library again. Your iPhone shows it too if iCloud Photos is on.</p>
<p>This web route has one more use. It shows whether the photo is truly gone. If the photo is missing here too, it is missing from iCloud, which tells you the backup route is next. It also shows the photo left every synced device at once. Deleting a photo on one device deletes it on all of them when iCloud Photos is on. Recover it once, and it returns everywhere.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Go to iCloud.com and sign in with your Apple Account.</li><li>Click Photos, then choose Recently Deleted.</li><li>Select a photo, then click Recover.</li></ol>
<h2>What to do when the photo is gone from Recently Deleted</h2>
<p>After 30 days, Recently Deleted empties itself. Your photo is gone from your phone, from iCloud.com, and from your Mac. No setting brings it back. Your last hope is an older backup, a full copy of your phone from one date. If that copy holds the photo, a restore brings it back. Check the date of your last backup first. Start there.</p>
<p>Find your backup date in Settings. Tap your name at the top, tap iCloud, then tap iCloud Backup to see the date of your last backup. Compare it with the day you deleted the photo. A backup from before that day may hold the photo. One from after that day will not. It copied your phone after the photo was gone.</p>
<p>Computer backups work the same way. Connect your iPhone to the Mac or PC you back up to. On a Mac with macOS 10.15 or later, open Finder, choose your iPhone, and click Restore from this backup. On older Macs and Windows PCs, open iTunes, click Restore Backup, and pick the backup from before that day. Your phone restarts and loads that copy.</p>

<h2>Why a backup restore is the last resort</h2>
<p>Restoring from a backup does not pick out one photo. It replaces your whole phone with the backup copy, so apps, messages, settings, and photos all roll back to that date. Anything you added after the backup date disappears. That trade is worth it for a lost wedding photo. It hurts for a month of new messages. Choose with care.</p>
<p>Save what is new before you start. Send recent photos to yourself, a friend, or a computer, and copy notes and files the same way. Write down any new passwords you set since the backup date. Once the restore runs, those new items are gone from the phone. A few minutes of saving now beats losing them twice.</p>
<p>To restore, erase the phone first. Go to Settings, tap General, then tap Transfer or Reset iPhone, and tap Erase All Content and Settings. Your phone restarts to the Hello screen. Choose Restore from iCloud Backup in setup. Sign in with your Apple Account and pick the backup by its date and size.</p>
<p>Stay on Wi-Fi and keep the phone plugged in while it restores. Photos keep downloading in the background after setup ends, and the app shows its progress at the bottom. Wait until it finishes before you judge the result. A half-synced library looks empty when it is only slow.</p>
<div class="note warn"><b>Common mistake:</b> Backing up now can replace the older backup that holds your photo. Restore first, then back up.</div>
<h2>Myths you can ignore about recovering iPhone photos</h2>
<p>Recovery apps fill the search results for this question. Most promise to pull deleted photos from your phone with one scan, yet no app can reach photos the phone has erased for good. A photo deleted more than 30 days ago is gone from the phone itself. Save your money and skip the scan. That scan finds nothing the phone has already erased. Never type your Apple Account password into a recovery app. Keep it for Apple sign-in screens only.</p>
<p>Shaking the phone or restarting it brings no photos back. It does not. A restart clears temporary glitches, not deletions, so if a photo is missing after a restart, check Recently Deleted next. Recently Deleted, not the restart, decides the result. The same goes for updating iOS. An update will not dig up a photo deleted months ago.</p>
<p>Removing a photo from an album saves nothing elsewhere, since albums are views of your library, not separate copies. Deleting a photo from any album deletes it from the whole library, and it lands in Recently Deleted either way. Albums never protect a photo from deletion. In a shared library, only the person who added a photo can recover it.</p>
<h2>A backup habit that saves the next deleted photo</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse4.mm.bing.net/th?q=iPhone+Settings+iCloud+Photos+toggle+turned+on&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="iPhone Settings iCloud Photos toggle turned on" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Think of recovery as a rescue, not a plan. A photo you save today is one you never have to chase. Turn on iCloud Photos so every shot syncs to Apple servers as you take it. Go to Settings, tap your name, tap iCloud, then tap Photos, and switch on Sync this iPhone. Your photos then live on your phone and in iCloud at once.</p>
<p>Make sure iCloud Backup runs too. Go to Settings, tap your name, tap iCloud, then tap iCloud Backup, and switch on Back Up This iPhone. It backs up each night on Wi-Fi while it charges. A fresh backup means a deleted photo is never more than a restore away. It also covers messages, settings, and apps.</p>
<p>Keep a second copy outside Apple too. Once a month, plug the phone into your computer and copy new photos to a folder, or use a USB-C flash drive made for iPhones. Keep the drive somewhere safe, away from the phone. Two copies in two places beat one copy in one cloud. That habit costs a few minutes a month. Worth it.</p>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Can I recover deleted photos on iPhone after 30 days?</h3>
<p>Only a backup helps after 30 days. Recently Deleted empties itself on day 30, and Apple says the photos cannot be brought back. Check for an iCloud or computer backup from an earlier date, then restore it to bring the photos back with the rest of the phone.</p>
<h3>Where is the Recently Deleted album on iPhone?</h3>
<p>It sits under Utilities in Photos. Swipe up from your photo grid to find it. Open Recently Deleted, then tap View Album. Use Face ID or Touch ID to open it. Older iPhones show the same album under the Albums tab instead.</p>
<h3>Do deleted photos stay in iCloud after I delete them on my iPhone?</h3>
<p>They stay for 30 days when iCloud Photos is on. Deleting a photo on your iPhone deletes it from iCloud and every synced device. Recover it within 30 days to bring it back everywhere at once. After 30 days, only a backup made before the deletion can help.</p>
<h3>Can I recover just one photo from an iCloud backup?</h3>
<p>No, Apple restores the whole backup or nothing. You erase the phone, then set it up from an older backup, so save any new photos and files first. Your single photo returns along with everything else from that date.</p>
<h3>Why can&#x27;t I find a photo I never deleted?</h3>
<p>Look in the Hidden album and check your iCloud Photos setting first. A photo hides when someone taps Hide, and it only appears in the Hidden album. If iCloud Photos is off, new shots stay on one device. Turn the setting on in Settings under your name, iCloud, then Photos.</p>
