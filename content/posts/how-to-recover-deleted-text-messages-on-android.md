<h2>How to recover deleted text messages on Android</h2>
<p>You deleted a text and want it back. Start with the hard truth. Google&#x27;s support pages for Android say a conversation deleted from all available copies cannot be recovered. That means it is not in any app Trash folder and not in a backup. Behavior differs between Google Messages, which in recent versions offers a Trash folder, and account-level backups. Samsung phones give you one more safety net. One made before the deletion gives you another. Start with the fastest check.</p>
<p>Stop sending new texts for a while. Every new message can overwrite that space. New texts, photos and downloads all write over it. That matters most if you plan a backup restore later. Switching the phone off is not needed, so just pause heavy use until you know which route you will take. No deleted message sits in a bin you can open at will. Hope lies in a copy made earlier.</p>
<p>Work out which messaging app you use before you tap anything. Most Android phones run Google Messages. Galaxy phones may run Samsung Messages instead, or both apps at once. Each app keeps its own trash folder and its own backup rules. Each set of steps below fits one app. Know yours first.</p>

<h2>Check the trash folder in Samsung Messages and Google Messages</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse4.mm.bing.net/th?q=hand+tapping+trash+icon+on+phone+screen&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="hand tapping trash icon on phone screen" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>On many recent Samsung phones, deleted threads go to a <strong>Trash</strong> folder. It typically retains items for around 30 days. Check your app&#x27;s Trash and the app&#x27;s help page for the exact period on your device. That window is your best chance. It costs nothing to check. In Samsung Messages, tap the three dots in the top corner. Then tap Trash. Deleted threads show there, with the date each one was removed. Touch and hold the thread you want back, then tap Restore and watch it return to your main list at once.</p>
<p>Recent Google Messages versions provide a Trash folder too. It usually retains deleted items for about 30 days. Confirm the limit in-app or in Google&#x27;s support documentation for your app version. Tap your profile picture in Google Messages, then look for Trash in the menu. Open it, touch and hold the conversation, and tap Restore. It arrives with app updates, so an older version may not show it. Update the app from the Play Store if Trash is missing. Then look again.</p>
<p>Do not confuse trash with the archive. Archiving hides a conversation. It does not delete it. For Google Messages, tap your profile picture, then tap Archived. Hold the conversation and tap Unarchive. Archived threads stay readable. Check this folder before you try anything harder.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Start in Samsung Messages on your phone.</li><li>Use the three dots in the top corner, then tap Trash.</li><li>Touch and hold the thread you want to bring back.</li><li>Choose Restore to return it to your main list.</li><li>Open the thread and check the messages are whole.</li></ol>
<h2>Restore deleted texts from a Google One backup</h2>
<p>Google account backups, exposed in Google One on many devices, can include SMS and MMS. Terminology and availability vary by device and region. RCS messages are included too when you use Google Messages. RCS means Rich Communication Services, the newer chat standard behind Google Messages. Backups run on their own. The phone must be idle, charging and on Wi-Fi. Restoring happens once, during the setup of a new phone or a phone you have just reset. That single fact decides whether this route helps you, so check the backup date before you touch anything else.</p>
<p>Check the backup before you reset anything. Find Backup in the Google One app and tap it. Read the backup details for the last backup date and the SMS listing, and check the date falls before the day you deleted the text. Any backup made after the deletion holds no copy of it. If no backup exists, stop here. A late date means the same thing. Resetting the phone will not bring the text back.</p>
<p>Resetting wipes the phone, so save anything new first. Photos, files and messages received after the backup date will be lost when you restore the older copy. Save them first. Copy photos to a computer. Then go to Settings &gt; System &gt; Reset options &gt; <strong>Erase all data (factory reset)</strong>. During setup, sign in with the same Google account. Choose a backup from the cloud and tick SMS and MMS messages. Your phone restores the backup as it stood on the backup date.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Look at the backup date and the SMS listing in the Google One app.</li><li>Move any new photos and files somewhere safe, such as a computer.</li><li>Go to Settings &gt; System &gt; Reset options &gt; Erase all data (factory reset).</li><li>Sign in with the same Google account during setup.</li><li>Choose the cloud backup when the setup screen offers it.</li><li>Tick SMS and MMS messages, then let the restore finish.</li></ol>
<h2>Restore deleted texts from a Samsung Cloud backup</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse3.mm.bing.net/th?q=Samsung+Galaxy+phone+settings+screen&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="Samsung Galaxy phone settings screen" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Galaxy owners get the same safety net through Samsung Cloud, with one big advantage: you can restore messages without wiping the phone. The maker&#x27;s own support page lays out both backup and restore. In Settings, tap Accounts and backup, then tap Back up data under Samsung Cloud. Turn on Messages if it is off. Run a fresh backup with Back up now. A fresh backup will not hold a text you already deleted.</p>
<p>To bring back a deleted text, go to Settings, then Accounts and backup, then <strong>Restore data</strong> under Samsung Cloud. Pick the device the backup came from, then check the date of that backup. That backup must be from before the deletion, or the text will not be in it. Select <strong>Messages</strong> only, so the restore touches nothing else. Hit Restore. Those threads return to Samsung Messages. This restore does not erase the phone first.</p>
<p>Smart Switch, Samsung&#x27;s own transfer and backup tool, can also hold a copy of your messages. If you backed up the phone to a computer with Smart Switch before the deletion, open Smart Switch on the computer. Connect the phone with a cable and choose Restore. Pick Messages from the list and let it finish. The backup lives on your computer, not in the cloud. Without a Smart Switch backup, this route has nothing to restore from.</p>

<h2>Myths about recovering deleted texts you can ignore</h2>
<p>Recovery apps fill the search results. Most of them promise more than they can deliver. These apps scan the phone&#x27;s storage for traces of deleted data. On modern Android phones, that scan rarely finds a whole text. Many need the phone <strong>rooted</strong>, which means switching off system protections most owners should leave alone. No recovery app can promise your message back. Skip them.</p>
<p>Your carrier is another dead end for most people. Carriers move texts across their network, but most do not hand customers a readable copy on request. Some carrier apps keep their own copy inside the app, so check yours if you used one. <strong>SIM cards</strong> hold no texts either. Modern phones store messages in the phone&#x27;s own storage, not on the SIM. Asking the carrier or swapping the SIM will not bring a deleted text back.</p>
<p>One screenshot or a second device can still save the day. Look for the same conversation on another phone or a linked computer. It may still be open in a tab even after the phone copy is gone. Ask the other person in the chat to resend the message or forward it. This simple ask works more often than any app. It costs nothing.</p>
<ul><li>Recovery app guarantee: no app can promise a deleted text back.</li><li>Carrier copy: most carriers do not provide readable message copies on request; policies vary, so check your carrier&#x27;s support.</li><li>SIM card storage: modern phones store texts in phone storage, not on the SIM.</li></ul>
<h2>Set up message backup so a deleted text is never lost again</h2>
<p>Set up the fix before you need it. Turn on Google backup now, while your messages are still there. In Settings, tap Google, then All services, then Backup. Turn on backup and check that SMS and MMS messages appear there. Tap <strong>Back up now</strong> and wait for it to finish. Google accounts generally include <strong>15 GB</strong> of shared free storage across Drive, Gmail and Photos. Message backups count against that quota, so large backups may need storage management or a Google One subscription.</p>
<p>Add Samsung Cloud too if you own a Galaxy phone. Head to Settings, then Accounts and backup, then Back up data under Samsung Cloud. Enable Messages. Review both backup dates once a month and after every update or SIM change. A backup that stopped running months ago protects nothing. Note the date first when a message goes missing. Check monthly.</p>
<p>Keep a second copy of the texts that matter most. Screenshot an address, a code or a confirmation and save it where you can find it. Forward a message that matters to your email. Or copy it into a note. They survive a deleted thread, a lost phone and a failed backup. Aim for this: no single deletion should ever take a message you cannot afford to lose. Start today.</p>
<ul><li>Google backup on: Settings &gt; Google &gt; All services &gt; Backup.</li><li>Samsung Cloud on: Settings &gt; Accounts and backup &gt; Back up data.</li><li>Backup date checked: both dates are recent and before today.</li><li>Key texts copied: screenshots or forwards saved outside Messages.</li></ul>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Check your backup date today. A backup from before the deletion is the only restore that works.</div>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Can I recover a text I deleted a year ago?</h3>
<p>Only if a backup from before that date still exists. Look at the backup date in the Google One app or in Samsung Cloud. A backup made after the deletion holds no copy of the text. If no old backup exists, there is nothing to restore from.</p>
<h3>Does a factory reset delete my photos too?</h3>
<p>A factory reset wipes the whole phone, including photos, files and apps. Save anything new before you reset. The restore brings back only what the backup held, so anything added after that date is lost unless you saved it first.</p>
<h3>Will restoring a backup delete my newer messages?</h3>
<p>The restore replaces your current messages with the version from the backup date. Messages sent or received after that date will be gone. Copy the newer threads you need before you reset.</p>
<h3>Can my carrier recover my deleted texts?</h3>
<p>No, in most cases. Carriers move texts across their network, but they rarely hand customers a readable copy. Some carrier apps keep their own copy, so check the carrier app if you used one. Your best routes stay the trash folder and a cloud backup.</p>
