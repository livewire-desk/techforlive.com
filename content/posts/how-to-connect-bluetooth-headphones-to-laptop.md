<h2>Connect Bluetooth headphones to your laptop in minutes</h2>
<p>Bluetooth is a short-range wireless link that lets two devices talk without a cable. Your laptop sends sound over that link, and the headphones play it. The link forms only when both sides agree to connect, so the laptop must be looking while the headphones offer. That offer is called pairing mode, and it is the step most people miss. Once the two sides agree, the laptop remembers the headphones and reconnects when they switch on nearby.</p>
<p>Pairing mode is a special state, not just switched on. Most headphones enter it when you hold the power button or a marked Bluetooth button for a few seconds. A light flashes, or a voice says pairing. The exact move differs by maker, so check the small guide in the box. Headphones often enter pairing mode on their own the first time they switch on. After that, they usually try to reconnect to the last device instead. The light usually blinks while the headphones wait, and turns steady once the laptop connects.</p>
<p>Charge the headphones before you start. A low battery can drop the link halfway through pairing, and the laptop then shows a device it cannot finish connecting.</p>

<h2>Pair Bluetooth headphones with a Windows 11 laptop</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse4.mm.bing.net/th?q=Windows+11+Bluetooth+settings+on+a+laptop+screen&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="Windows 11 Bluetooth settings on a laptop screen" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>All Bluetooth controls in Windows 11 live in one place. Press the Windows key and I together, and Settings opens. Click Bluetooth &amp; devices in the menu on the left. The switch at the top turns the laptop&#x27;s Bluetooth on and off. Leave it on, and leave Settings open while you work.</p>
<p>Quick settings give a faster route through the icons at the bottom right of the screen. Click the Network, Sound or Battery icon to open the panel, then click the arrow beside the Bluetooth tile. Headphones that are offering a connection should appear under New devices. Click the name to pair. Some headphones show a pop-up instead. Swift Pair is a Windows prompt that appears when a supported device is nearby, so click Connect on it.</p>
<p>The pairing should connect the headphones at once. Windows shows the headphones as Connected in the list. If the name shows under Your devices but sound still comes from the speakers, the output choice is the problem. The sound section below fixes it. A laptop without Bluetooth shows no switch at all in these menus. Microsoft says a USB Bluetooth adapter can add Bluetooth to such a laptop, and that adapter plugs into a spare USB port.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Hold the pairing button until the light flashes.</li><li>Press the Windows key and I together to open Settings, then click Bluetooth &amp; devices in the left menu.</li><li>Make sure the Bluetooth switch at the top is On.</li><li>Click Add device, then click Bluetooth.</li><li>Click the headphones when their name appears, then click Done when Windows says the device is ready.</li></ol>
<div class="note"><b>Check this:</b> Play a song right after pairing. Sound from the speakers means the output choice still needs changing.</div>
<h2>Pair Bluetooth headphones with a Mac laptop</h2>
<p>Every Bluetooth device on a Mac lives on one settings page. Click the Apple menu at the top left of the screen, then choose System Settings. Click Bluetooth in the sidebar, and scroll down if you cannot see it. Make sure Bluetooth is switched on at the top of the page. Headphones appear in the list once they are in pairing mode and close enough for the Mac to hear them.</p>
<p>AirPods follow the same page with one extra step at the start. Put both AirPods in the charging case and open the lid. For most AirPods, press and hold the setup button on the back of the case until the status light flashes white. If your case has no button, follow the leaflet in the box. Click Connect beside the AirPods name in the list.</p>
<p>Give the first pairing a few seconds. A small prompt may ask you to accept or type a short code. Type the code the headphones show, or click Accept when macOS asks. The headphones then move to the top of the list with Connected beside the name. If sound still comes from the Mac speakers, the headphones paired fine but the Mac has not picked them as its output yet.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Hold the pairing button until the light flashes, or put AirPods in the open case and hold the setup button until the light flashes white.</li><li>Click the Apple menu, choose System Settings, then click Bluetooth in the sidebar.</li><li>Make sure Bluetooth is switched on.</li><li>Hover over the headphones in the list and click Connect.</li><li>Click Accept or type the code if macOS asks, then play a sound to test the link.</li></ol>

<h2>Pick the headphones as your sound output</h2>
<p>Getting paired is only half the job. A paired device can sit in the list while the laptop keeps playing sound through its own speakers. Laptops treat pairing and sound output as two separate choices, and the second one decides where the sound goes. Open Sound settings once after pairing, and you will not have to guess which device is playing.</p>
<p>Windows sends sound to the device picked under Output. Open Settings, then go to System &gt; Sound, and look at the Output list near the top. Choose the headphones there. From the taskbar, click the speaker icon, then the small arrow beside the volume slider, and pick the headphones. The change takes effect at once. If the headphones vanish from the Output list, they have dropped the Bluetooth link, so pair them again.</p>
<p>Mac users pick the output in a matching place. Click the Apple menu, choose System Settings, then click Sound in the sidebar. Choose Output, then select the headphones from the list. A headset with a microphone is two devices in one, so check the Input tab too when calls pick up the wrong mic.</p>

<h2>When the headphones will not pair or no sound comes out</h2>
<p>Most failed pairings have a plain cause, and the fix is quick once you know which one it is. The most common cause is a headphone that is switched on but not discoverable. Such headphones do not show up in any list, because the laptop cannot hear them. Hold the pairing button until the light flashes, then look at the laptop list again. A phone that already holds the link is the second common cause. Switch Bluetooth off on the phone, or disconnect the headphones there first.</p>
<p>Distance causes the next group of faults. Bluetooth is a short-range link, and a room or two between the laptop and the headphones is enough to break it. Bring the headphones close, and move away from a microwave or a thick metal desk if the sound stutters. Restarting both sides clears the rest: switch the headphones off and on, then turn the laptop&#x27;s Bluetooth off and back on.</p>
<p>A stalled pairing needs a fresh start. If the headphones appear in the list but the click does nothing, remove them and pair again. On Windows, open the Bluetooth settings page, click the device and choose Remove device. On a Mac, open System Settings &gt; Bluetooth and remove the device from the list. Pair again with the steps above. A driver update can also help on Windows when the laptop cannot see any Bluetooth device at all. Look on the laptop maker&#x27;s support page for a Bluetooth driver that matches your model.</p>
<p>To switch back to your phone later, disconnect the headphones from the laptop first, then pick them from the phone&#x27;s Bluetooth list. A Mac that shows no Bluetooth switch at all may need a restart first, then a macOS update under System Settings &gt; General &gt; Software Update.</p>
<ul><li>Flashing light first: wait for the light to flash before you look at the laptop list.</li><li>One device at a time: disconnect the headphones from your phone before pairing with the laptop.</li><li>Close and charged: keep the headphones near the laptop with enough battery to finish pairing.</li><li>Fresh pairing: remove the old pairing and pair again when a saved device will not connect.</li></ul>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Never install a driver from a pop-up or a site you do not know. Use the laptop maker&#x27;s own support page only.</div>
<h2>Myths about Bluetooth headphones you can ignore</h2>
<p>One belief says Bluetooth headphones drain a laptop battery fast, so people switch Bluetooth off between uses. A connected but silent pair draws far less than the display, so switching Bluetooth off to save battery rarely shows up in a day&#x27;s use. The battery you save by switching it off is too small to notice in a normal day.</p>
<p>Another belief says you must buy the same brand as your laptop for the headphones to work. Bluetooth is a shared standard, so headphones from one maker pair with a laptop from another. The Bluetooth SIG, the group that looks after the standard, sets the rules every maker follows. Brand extras such as special apps or quick-pair prompts may only work with the same brand, but plain pairing and sound work across brands.</p>
<p>A third belief says pairing once means the headphones will always connect on their own. Most headphones do reconnect to the last device, but only when that device is near and its Bluetooth is on. A phone in your pocket often wins the race, because the headphones remember it from last time. Disconnect the headphones from the phone, or switch the phone&#x27;s Bluetooth off, and the laptop gets its turn.</p>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Why won&#x27;t my laptop find my Bluetooth headphones?</h3>
<p>The headphones are probably not in pairing mode, so the laptop cannot see them. Hold the pairing button until the light flashes, then look again. They may also be linked to your phone, so disconnect them there first.</p>
<h3>Can I connect Bluetooth headphones to a laptop without Bluetooth?</h3>
<p>Yes, with a USB Bluetooth adapter. Plug the adapter into a spare USB port and Windows should show the Bluetooth switch in Settings. Microsoft says such an adapter can add Bluetooth to a Windows device that has none built in.</p>
<h3>Why do my headphones connect but no sound comes out?</h3>
<p>Pairing alone does not change where sound plays. Open Sound settings, look under Output, and click the headphones. The change takes effect at once, so play any sound to check it.</p>
<h3>Can Bluetooth headphones connect to two devices at once?</h3>
<p>Some can, through a feature makers call multipoint, but many cannot. Check the guide that came with yours. If yours does not support it, disconnect from the first device before pairing with the second.</p>
