<h2>How to check data usage on iPhone in one minute</h2>
<p>An iPhone keeps a running count of the mobile data it uses, and the screen that shows it sits two taps from the home screen. Open the Settings app, then tap Cellular. In some countries the same menu is called Mobile Service or Mobile Data, but it works the same way. Near the top sits your total, and the list below breaks it down app by app.</p>
<p>This total answers the question most people actually ask: how much data have I used. Measured in <strong>megabytes</strong> or <strong>gigabytes</strong>, it covers everything the phone sent or received over the mobile network since the counter was last reset. Wi-Fi does not count toward it. Calls and texts do not count either, because they run on a different part of the network.</p>
<p>Below the total sits a list you read rather than tap through, with a figure printed under every app name. To stop an app using mobile data at all, switch off the toggle beside its name, and from then on it can only use Wi-Fi. Use that switch for a video app that keeps eating data in the background, and turn it back on just as fast. On a phone with two lines, the screen shows the data for the line selected for mobile data, so check which line is selected before you read the numbers.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Open the Settings app on your iPhone.</li><li>Tap Cellular, or Mobile Service or Mobile Data, depending on your country.</li><li>Read the number beside Current Period for your total.</li><li>Scroll down and tap Show All to see every app and its figure.</li></ol>

<h2>What Current Period really counts, and what it misses</h2>
<p>Current Period is the label beside your total, and it is the part of the screen people misunderstand most. This month is not what it means, and neither is your billing cycle. Instead it means the time since somebody last tapped Reset Statistics on this phone. Whether it was last week or two years ago, the number grows the whole time without ever starting over on its own.</p>
<p>Scroll to the very bottom of the Cellular screen and you will see <strong>Last Reset</strong>, with the date and time the counter last started from zero. Only that date tells you what the total actually covers. If it says a date six months back, your total is six months of data, and comparing it with a monthly allowance tells you nothing. Resetting gives you a number you can actually use.</p>
<p>Carriers keep a separate count, and that one follows your billing cycle, not your phone. Apple says the carrier has the most accurate figure for a current period, because the carrier measures what crossed its network. Your phone counts what its own apps used, and the two can differ by a little. Use the phone to find which apps used the data, and use the carrier to find how much of your plan is left.</p>
<h2>Which apps are eating your data</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse3.mm.bing.net/th?q=iPhone+screen+listing+apps+with+data+usage+figures&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="iPhone screen listing apps with data usage figures" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Apps that used the most sit at the top of the list, so the culprit is usually the first name you see. Video apps, social feeds and cloud photo backups sit there most often, because they move large files without asking. A music app that downloads songs for offline listening can climb the list too, even though each song is small, because the downloads add up over weeks.</p>
<p>At the bottom of the short list, Show All reveals every app, not just the first few. Each entry shows what that app used on mobile data only, so an app that mostly runs on Wi-Fi can still show a small number here. Small as it is, that number is the point: it tells you what the app cost you when you were away from Wi-Fi, which is the only part that touches your plan. An app with a tiny figure may simply have spent weeks on Wi-Fi, not that it uses little data overall.</p>
<p>System Services sits above the app list and covers the phone&#x27;s own jobs: software updates, backups, location services and the like. Tap the row to see the breakdown by service. No switch exists for a single system service. Next to a video app, the total there is usually small, and if it ever looks large, the likely cause is a software update that downloaded over mobile data while you were out.</p>
<h2>Check how much data you have left with your carrier</h2>
<p>What your iPhone cannot tell you is what is left. What you used is all it knows, not what you bought. Allowance, billing date and remaining balance all live with your carrier, and you will find them in the carrier&#x27;s app or on its website. Most carriers show a simple bar or a number on the home screen of their app, next to your plan name.</p>
<p>Open your carrier&#x27;s app and sign in, or log in on its website, and look for a section called data usage, my plan or account. Your bill is based on that figure, and it is the one to trust when the phone and the carrier disagree. Without an app, your carrier&#x27;s website works the same way, and a call to its support line gets the same number read out to you.</p>
<p>Check the remaining balance a few days before your billing cycle ends, not on the last day. Checking early leaves time to switch on Low Data Mode - Settings &gt; Cellular &gt; Cellular Data Options &gt; Data Mode - or hold off a big download until the cycle renews. A carrier app can also show whether a top-up or an add-on is cheaper than paying for extra data. The choice depends on your plan, so read the terms on the screen before you buy anything.</p>

<h2>Reset the counter so next month starts clean</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse3.mm.bing.net/th?q=how+to+check+data+usage+on+iphone+Reset+counter+so+next+month&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="how to check data usage on iphone - Reset the counter so next month starts clean" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Resetting the counter does not change your plan or delete anything. Only the phone&#x27;s count goes back to zero, so the next total you see covers the time after the reset. Useful numbers need a start date: reset it on the day your billing cycle starts, and the phone&#x27;s total will track your month, roughly, until the cycle ends.</p>
<p>Look for the button at the very bottom of the Cellular screen, under a heading that reads Cellular Usage Statistics, with the Last Reset date beside it. Tap Reset Statistics, then confirm. Every app&#x27;s total drops to zero at the same time, and the list starts filling again as apps use data. There is no undo, so check the old total first if you still want it.</p>
<p>Pick a day you will remember, such as the first of the month, and reset on that day each month. A monthly reminder on the phone does the job, and after two or three cycles the habit sticks. Reset at a random time and the number still works, but you have to remember the date before it means anything.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Open Settings, then tap Cellular.</li><li>Scroll all the way to the bottom of the screen.</li><li>Note the Last Reset date so you know what the old total covered.</li><li>Tap Reset Statistics, then tap it again to confirm.</li></ol>
<h2>Common mistakes that make the numbers look wrong</h2>
<p>Most readers trip up by reading Current Period as this month&#x27;s usage when the counter was last reset long ago. A total of <strong>40 gigabytes</strong> looks alarming until you notice the Last Reset date is eight months back, which makes it about five gigabytes a month. Always read the date before you read the number, and reset the counter if the date is old.</p>
<p><code>Wi-Fi Assist</code> is the second trap, and it is on by default. When Wi-Fi is weak, the phone quietly switches to mobile data to keep you connected, and that data counts toward your total. You will find it at the bottom of the Cellular screen. Turning it off means a weak Wi-Fi signal may stall instead of switching over, so the trade-off is a slower connection against a smaller data bill.</p>
<p>Roaming data and hotspot data catch people out too. Data used while roaming is counted separately on the screen, and it can cost more depending on your plan, so check it before a trip rather than after. Data shared through Personal Hotspot also counts toward your phone&#x27;s total, because the other device is using your connection. If the total jumps after a weekend away, the hotspot is the first place to look.</p>
<ul><li>Last Reset date: read it before trusting the total.</li><li>Carrier app: check what is left, not just what you used.</li><li>Wi-Fi Assist: decide whether weak Wi-Fi should cost you data.</li><li>Roaming: check the roaming figure before you travel.</li></ul>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Why does my iPhone say I used more data than my carrier?</h3>
<p>Each number covers a different window, so a gap between them is normal. The phone&#x27;s count can stretch back months if it was never reset, while the carrier&#x27;s covers a single billing cycle. A reset at the start of your cycle puts the two figures on the same footing.</p>
<h3>Can I see data usage for a single day?</h3>
<p>Not on the phone itself. One running total since the last reset is all the Cellular screen shows, with no daily breakdown. Reset the counter in the morning and check it at night for a single day&#x27;s figure, or look in your carrier&#x27;s app, which sometimes shows daily use.</p>
<h3>Does resetting statistics affect my plan?</h3>
<p>No. Reset Statistics only clears the count on your phone, and it changes nothing with your carrier. Your plan, your allowance and your bill stay exactly as they were. Think of the reset as a way to make the phone&#x27;s number cover a period you choose.</p>
<h3>What is System Services data?</h3>
<p>System Services covers the data used by the phone&#x27;s own jobs, such as software updates, backups and location services. You can see the breakdown by tapping System Services above the app list. Switching mobile data off for one system service on its own is not possible.</p>
