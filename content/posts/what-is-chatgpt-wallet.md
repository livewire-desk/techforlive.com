<h2>What is ChatGPT Wallet?</h2>
<p>OpenAI announced ChatGPT Wallet in November 2026, and Reuters covered the launch that week. OpenAI says it worked with card networks to build the wallet. It lets an AI agent finish a purchase you asked for, using a card you added earlier.</p>
<p>The agent is called a dot. OpenAI describes dots as always-on helpers. Each dot has its own cloud computer and browser, and it works on tasks while you do other things. A dot can search the web, compare products and fill in a checkout form. Wallet adds the last step: paying.</p>
<p>Wallet is not a bank account. It holds no money and has no balance to top up. It is a card wallet. It stores the card you choose and hands the shop a stand-in number at checkout. Your rewards and your card&#x27;s dispute cover stay as they are for eligible purchases.</p>
<p>It works like this. You describe what you want. The dot finds it, checks the price and builds the order. You check the total and approve it with your face or fingerprint. The dot cannot pay without that approval. That is the whole point of the design.</p>

<h2>How a ChatGPT Wallet purchase works</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse3.mm.bing.net/th?q=Phone+screen+showing+a+payment+approval+with+total+and+card&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="Phone screen showing a payment approval with total and card" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Each purchase runs in four moves. The card goes in once, and after that you only approve. Each approval is tied to one order, one total and one card. Nothing is charged in the background.</p>
<p>A payment token is a substitute card number with its own security code and expiry date. It works for that one purchase. The merchant and the dot never see the number printed on your plastic. Your real details stay with the card network.</p>
<p>Approval is not a receipt. OpenAI says approving a purchase does not mean the order went through. Wait for the merchant&#x27;s confirmation, and check your card statement. If no confirmation arrives, contact the shop first.</p>
<p>Behind the scenes, the dot works like a shopper with a browser. It searches the open web, reads merchant pages and fills in the checkout form. You see none of that. What you see is the approval screen, and that screen is the only part that moves money.</p>
<p>Learn the flow once and it stays the same each time. Add the card, ask the dot, read the screen, approve with your face or finger. If any line on the approval screen looks wrong, stop and ask the dot to fix it.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Add a card once. Open Settings, then Wallet, then Add Card on your phone, or ask your dot to help you add a card. Type the number, expiry date and security code into the secure form. Never type card details into the chat itself.</li><li>Ask your dot to buy something. Tell it what you want and any limits, such as a budget or a shop you trust. The dot searches, compares and builds the order.</li><li>Read the approval screen. It shows the merchant, the items, the total, the card and any shipping or recurring terms. Check every line before you go on.</li><li>Approve with Face ID or a fingerprint. The card network then issues a payment token. The shop charges the token, not your card.</li></ol>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Ask the dot to show you the approval screen before you approve anything. Read the total, the merchant and the card the same way you would read a paper receipt.</div>
<h2>Who can use ChatGPT Wallet right now</h2>
<p>This launch is deliberately small. OpenAI says it is starting with a narrow group so it can learn from real use. The rollout is gradual, so an eligible user may not see Wallet yet. OpenAI has given no date for a wider release.</p>
<p>OpenAI&#x27;s launch notes list the supported card types, and some banks may ask you to verify during setup. Check your bank for details before you add a card, so you are not stuck halfway through. The setup is quick once the card is accepted, and you do it only once.</p>
<p>Some shops may block agent checkouts. OpenAI admits that a merchant&#x27;s site can refuse a purchase made through Wallet. If a shop will not take it, you pay the usual way. Which shops block it is not published.</p>
<p>Every limit below is checked at setup. Miss one and Wallet will not appear. OpenAI and the card networks set them together, not your phone. A Plus plan, a non-US address or a debit card each stops the setup on its own. Check the list before you hunt through Settings for a Wallet menu that is not there.</p>

<h2>What stays safe, and what to check before you approve</h2>
<p>Keep the record of each approval. The dot shows you what you approved, and that record is what you compare against your statement. If a charge differs from the approval, that record is your evidence. A quick check each week catches a wrong charge early.</p>
<p>Your card&#x27;s own cover stays in place. OpenAI says existing card rewards still apply, and dispute cover still applies for eligible purchases. A faulty item is still the merchant&#x27;s problem first, and your issuer&#x27;s second. Wallet changes how you pay, not what your card promises.</p>
<p>Check the approval screen like a till receipt. The merchant name, the items, the total and the card must all match what you asked for. Look for a shipping charge or a subscription term hiding at the bottom. The screen is your last chance to catch a wrong item. A wrong total approved is still your bill to pay.</p>
<p>Know where to go if it goes wrong. Order problems go to the merchant. A charge you do not recognise goes to your card issuer. A Wallet error, or a charge different from the one you approved, goes to OpenAI Support.</p>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Recurring terms carry forward. If you approve a plan with monthly billing, the merchant may keep charging the token under the terms you accepted. Read the renewal line before you approve.</div>
<h2>Mistakes to avoid with ChatGPT Wallet</h2>
<p>Typing your card number into the chat is the biggest mistake. The dot will never ask for it there. Card details go only in the secure form inside Settings, then Wallet. That form is the only place your card details ever go. If a message asks for your number in plain text, close it and report it.</p>
<p>Trusting the dot&#x27;s pick without looking is the second mistake. The dot chooses the shop and the item, and it can pick the wrong size, colour or seller. Read the items on the approval screen. A wrong pick approved is still your order.</p>
<p>Assuming Wallet works everywhere is the third mistake. It does not work in ordinary ChatGPT, in Work threads or in Codex. It does not work outside the United States. Each of those will simply fail, and the fix is to pay the usual way.</p>
<p>Do not treat approval as a guarantee. Your tap approves the payment, not the order. The merchant can still decline it, run out of stock or fail to confirm. Check for the confirmation email before you count the item as bought.</p>

<h2>Should you turn ChatGPT Wallet on?</h2>

<p>Wallet suits one reader well: a US Pro subscriber who already asks a dot to shop. If the dot finds your groceries, books your travel or reorders your supplies, Wallet saves the last fiddly step. The approval screen keeps you in charge of the money.</p>
<p>It is a poor fit for everyone else right now. If you are outside the United States, on Plus or on a free plan, Wallet is simply not open to you. If you like choosing each shop yourself, the dot adds little. If the idea of an agent spending makes you uneasy, wait.</p>
<p>OpenAI plans to loosen the reins later. It says users should one day be able to pre-approve purchases inside rules they set: spending limits, purchase categories or time periods. Those controls are not live at launch. Today, every purchase still needs your face or your finger.</p>
<p>Media reports say OpenAI does not take a fee on Wallet transactions at launch. That tells you where the value sits for now: OpenAI wants the habit, not the fee. Watch what changes when the pre-approval controls arrive. That is when the real test of trust begins.</p>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Is ChatGPT Wallet a bank account?</h3>
<p>It is not. Wallet stores a card you already own and hands the shop a one-off token at checkout. Your money stays with your bank, and your card&#x27;s rewards and dispute cover still apply for eligible purchases.</p>
<h3>Does the agent see my card number?</h3>
<p>It does not. The card number, expiry date and security code go into a secure form, never into the chat. After you approve, the card network issues a token with its own number, code and expiry date. The dot and the merchant see only that token.</p>
<h3>Can ChatGPT Wallet buy things without asking me?</h3>
<p>Not at launch. Every purchase needs your review and a Face ID or fingerprint check. OpenAI says pre-approval inside spending limits and categories may come later, but it has given no date. Today, no approval means no charge.</p>
<h3>Why can&#x27;t I see Wallet in my ChatGPT app?</h3>
<p>The rollout is gradual and narrow. OpenAI&#x27;s documentation lists the current eligibility rules, so check the official Wallet help page if you do not see it. You also need an eligible US-issued card, and it only works inside a dot conversation. If any of those do not match, Wallet will not show up.</p>
<h3>What happens if the order goes wrong?</h3>
<p>Start with the merchant for anything about the order itself, such as a late delivery or a wrong item. Talk to your card issuer about a charge you do not recognise. Contact OpenAI Support only for Wallet errors, or when the charge differs from the one you approved.</p>
