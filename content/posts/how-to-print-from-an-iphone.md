<h2>How to print from an iPhone with AirPrint</h2>
<p>AirPrint is Apple&#x27;s built-in way to print from an iPhone, and it needs nothing installed on your phone. Your iPhone finds printers on your Wi-Fi network by itself. The whole job takes a handful of taps once the printer is set up. That setup happens once, on the printer, not on your phone. No software to install. Apple&#x27;s AirPrint support page lists these same steps.</p>
<p>Start by opening the thing you want to print. It can be a photo in Photos, a web page in Safari, a file in Files, or an email in Mail. Tap the Share button, the square with an arrow pointing up. Scroll down the list and tap Print. Some apps hide Print behind a three-dot button or a reply button instead.</p>
<p>Tap No Printer Selected at the top of the print screen. Your iPhone lists every AirPrint printer on the same Wi-Fi network. Pick yours. The list can take a few seconds to fill while your iPhone searches the network, so wait before you tap anything else. A printer that never appears is usually on a different network or asleep.</p>
<p>Set the number of copies and the pages you want, since photos offer size and paper choices while documents offer a page range. Tap Print in the upper-right corner and the job leaves your phone. Printing starts within a few moments when the printer is awake and has paper. With no printer near, pinch out on the preview page to open it as a PDF, then save it to Files.</p>

<h2>What has to be true before AirPrint works</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=wireless+printer+on+desk+near+home+router&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="wireless printer on desk near home router" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Three things must line up. All three matter. Your printer must support AirPrint. It must sit on your Wi-Fi network. Your iPhone must use that same network. Miss one and the printer stays hidden. Check them in that order and you find the fault fast.</p>
<p>Look for the word AirPrint on the printer box, in its manual, or on the maker&#x27;s product page for your model. Check the spec sheet for your exact model. If the word is missing from the spec sheet, assume the printer lacks AirPrint and use the maker app instead. A printer with no Wi-Fi at all cannot use AirPrint.</p>
<p>Open Settings, then Wi-Fi, and read the network name with the check mark beside it, since that is the network your phone uses right now. Now check the printer. Its screen or its app shows its own network name. The two names must match exactly, including the 5 GHz and 2.4 GHz versions of one router. An office printer on a separate network segment can stay hidden even when the names match.</p>
<p>A printer wired to your router with a network cable also works. Apple says AirPrint runs over Wi-Fi or a wired network connection. A printer plugged into a computer by USB does not count, and neither does one linked to a computer by Bluetooth. Both need the network link.</p>
<ul><li>Printer supports AirPrint: check the box, manual, or maker page.</li><li>Printer on Wi-Fi: joined to your home network.</li><li>iPhone on the same network: check Settings, then Wi-Fi.</li><li>Paper loaded and no error light showing on the printer.</li></ul>
<h2>Where the Print button hides in each app</h2>
<p>Every app puts Print in a slightly different place, though the Share button is the usual door into the print menu. In Photos you open a picture and tap Share, then Print. To print several photos, tap Select in the library, tap each one, then Share and Print.</p>
<p>Safari hides Print under Share too, so open the page, tap Share, scroll down the action list, and tap Print. Mail works differently. Open the message, tap the Reply button, and choose Print from that menu. An attachment prints after you tap it open, then use the Share button inside the preview to reach Print.</p>
<p>Notes and Files follow the same Share pattern, so open the note or file, tap Share, and tap Print. Pages and other Apple apps do the same. Third-party apps vary. Some show Print under a three-dot menu. Others put it under an Actions button. Gmail, Google Docs, and Outlook each put Print under their own menu button. Check the three dots inside the app when the Share sheet has no Print option. If Print never appears in the Share sheet, scroll to the bottom of the actions list and tap Edit Actions, then add Print. An app with no Print option cannot print that item at all. Try another app.</p>
<p>Messages has no Print option for a conversation. Save a photo from the chat first, then print it from Photos. A long text thread prints best after you copy it into Notes. Screenshots print from Photos like any other picture.</p>

<h2>No AirPrint printer? Use the maker app instead</h2>
<p>Older printers often lack AirPrint. The maker usually ships a free iPhone app that fills the gap. HP calls its app HP Smart. Epson calls its app Epson iPrint. Canon calls its app Canon PRINT Inkjet/SELPHY. Brother calls its app Brother iPrint&amp;Scan. Each one talks to its own brand over Wi-Fi.</p>
<p>Install the app for your brand from the App Store, then open it and let it search for printers on your Wi-Fi network. Tap Add Printer if nothing appears on its own. The app guides you through the rest. Allow Local Network when iOS asks, or the app cannot see the printer.</p>
<p>Pick Print Photos or Print Documents inside the app, then choose the file from your phone or a cloud drive. Set paper size, quality, and copies. Tap Print. That is the whole job. The app also shows ink levels and printer errors, which AirPrint never does. That extra detail helps when a job fails.</p>
<p>Some printers offer Wi-Fi Direct, a mode where the printer makes its own network for your phone to join. Open Settings, then Wi-Fi, on your iPhone and pick the printer network. Print the usual way, then switch back to your home network after. This mode suits a printer with no router nearby.</p>

<h2>When the printer does not show up</h2>
<p>The most common failure is a printer that never appears. Start with the network. Check that the printer and the phone share one Wi-Fi name, then restart the printer, the phone, and the router, in that order. Each restart takes a minute. Try again after each one.</p>
<p>A VPN on the iPhone can block printer discovery. Turn it off and try again. The printer may also sit on a guest network. Guest networks often block devices from seeing each other. Move the printer to the main network. A sleeping printer can miss the first search, so wake it first.</p>
<p>Check paper, ink, and error lights before you blame the phone, since a printer with an empty tray or a jam reports nothing to AirPrint. It just stays silent. Clear the error on the printer itself. Ink warnings do not always stop a print, but a paper jam always does.</p>
<p>Update the printer firmware when the maker offers it. Old firmware drops off Wi-Fi without warning. The maker&#x27;s app usually offers the update. Keep your iPhone on the latest iOS version too. Apple fixes AirPrint discovery in updates from time to time.</p>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Never pay for a print app that promises to fix discovery. Free maker apps and AirPrint cover nearly every home printer.</div>
<h2>Mistakes that waste paper, ink, and time</h2>
<p>Printing a whole web page wastes paper, since Safari prints the page as it looks, ads and all. Use Reader view first if the page offers it. Print only the range you need, for example pages 3 to 4, using the Range field.</p>
<p>Photos print darker than they look on screen. Phone screens glow. Paper does not. Raise brightness slightly before you print a dark photo. Check that single page before you print the rest. Colour ink costs more than black, so pick black and white for drafts.</p>
<p>A router update can split your networks apart again. The job looks sent and then nothing happens, because the phone and printer must share the network every time. Check the names after any router change, and rejoin the printer to the home network when they drift apart.</p>
<p>Paper size trips people up more than any setting. A document set to A4 prints cropped on Letter paper, and the reverse crops the other way. Check the size in the print options before you tap Print. Match it to the paper sitting in the tray. One test page shows the problem fast.</p>
<ul><li>Check the page count: print only the pages you need.</li><li>Check one page first: look at colour and layout before the rest.</li><li>Use black and white: drafts do not need colour ink.</li><li>Check the network names: phone and printer must match.</li></ul>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Do I need to install a printer on my iPhone?</h3>
<p>No. AirPrint needs nothing installed and no setup on the phone. Your iPhone finds AirPrint printers on your Wi-Fi network each time you print. You only set the printer up on Wi-Fi once, using its own screen or the maker&#x27;s app.</p>
<h3>Can I print without a Wi-Fi router?</h3>
<p>Yes, if the printer can make its own hotspot. Join that network in Settings, then Wi-Fi, print, and switch back to your home network when done.</p>
<h3>Why does my printer show as offline or missing?</h3>
<p>Offline usually means the printer left the network or went to sleep. Wake the printer and check it shares your phone&#x27;s Wi-Fi name. Power-cycle the printer, the phone, and the router, one at a time.</p>
<h3>Can I print from an iPhone to any printer?</h3>
<p>Nearly any printer works one way or another. AirPrint covers most wireless printers. Older models print through the maker&#x27;s free app instead. A printer with no Wi-Fi at all needs a cable to a computer, and the iPhone cannot reach it directly.</p>
<h3>How do I cancel a print job I just sent?</h3>
<p>Open the App Switcher while the job runs and tap Print Center, then tap Cancel Printing. Jobs already on paper cannot be stopped, so cancel fast when you spot the wrong file.</p>
