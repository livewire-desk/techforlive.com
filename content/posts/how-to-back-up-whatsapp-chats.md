<h2>How to back up WhatsApp chats: the short answer</h2>
<p>WhatsApp does not keep your chats on its servers. Your messages live on your phone, so if the phone is lost, stolen or wiped, the chats go with it, unless you backed them up. The backup is a copy of your chats in the cloud: your Google Account on Android, iCloud on iPhone. Set it once, and it runs on its own from then on.</p>
<p>The menu is the same on both phones. Open WhatsApp, tap Settings, then Chats, then Chat backup. That one screen holds everything: the last backup, the next one, the account it goes to, and the schedule. The date and size under Last backup tell you the copy landed. The steps below walk through each phone in turn. If you only want the quick version, tap Back up on that screen now, then set the schedule to Daily.</p>
<p>Two things catch people out. A backup is tied to your phone number and your cloud account. You need the same number and the same account to get it back. And a backup is not a readable copy: you cannot open it and read old messages outside WhatsApp.</p>

<h2>How to back up WhatsApp on Android</h2>
<p>Android backs up to the Google Account you choose. Before the first backup, check three things. A Google Account signed in on the phone, Google Play services installed, and enough free room in that Google Account. A steady connection helps, and Wi-Fi is best for a first backup, which can be large. The free tier of a Google Account is 15 GB, shared with Gmail, Google Drive and Google Photos.</p>
<p>Starting the backup takes a minute. Open WhatsApp, tap the three dots in the top corner, then Settings, then Chats, then Chat backup. Tap Back up to your Google Account and pick a schedule other than Never. Then tap Back up to run one straight away, and wait for it to finish before you close the app.</p>
<p>Three settings on that screen are worth a look. Include videos decides whether clips go into the backup, and they can double its size. Back up using cellular lets it run without Wi-Fi, at the cost of your data plan. On Android, the backup counts against your Google Account storage; on iPhone, against your iCloud storage.</p>

<h2>How to back up WhatsApp on iPhone</h2>
<p>On iPhone, WhatsApp backs up to iCloud. Before you start, sign in to iCloud with iCloud Drive turned on, and let WhatsApp use it. The path is iPhone Settings, then your name, then iCloud, then iCloud Drive. iCloud&#x27;s free tier is 5 GB, and your backup shares that room with everything else Apple stores for you. If the backup will not fit, free up space or buy more before you begin.</p>
<p>iPhone follows the same steps. Open WhatsApp and tap Settings at the bottom right, then Chats, then Chat backup. Tap Back Up Now for an immediate copy, then set Auto Backup to Daily, Weekly or Monthly. Daily keeps the copy freshest. WhatsApp&#x27;s Help Center says to keep free space of at least 2.05 times the size of the backup, both in iCloud and on the iPhone. A big backup needs room in both places.</p>
<p>One iPhone quirk: WhatsApp can also sit inside the iPhone&#x27;s own iCloud Backup, which copies the whole phone. That is a second safety net, not a replacement for the WhatsApp backup itself. To restore chats from it, you restore the whole phone. Keep both on if you have the room. If space is tight, the WhatsApp backup is the one that matters for your chats.</p>
<h2>Make the backup private: end-to-end encryption</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse3.mm.bing.net/th?q=padlock+on+a+phone+screen&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="padlock on a phone screen" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>A normal WhatsApp backup is not protected by WhatsApp&#x27;s end-to-end encryption. Google or Apple holds the copy, and their rules decide who can read it. That is fine for most people, and it is how backups have always worked. If you want the backup locked so only you can open it, WhatsApp offers end-to-end encrypted backup, and it takes a minute to switch on.</p>
<p>Find the switch on the same screen. Go to Settings, then Chats, then Chat backup, then End-to-end encrypted backup, and tap Turn on. WhatsApp asks you to protect it with a password you make, or a 64-digit key it generates for you. Newer versions offer a passkey instead. Pick the one you will not lose, and keep it somewhere safe, away from the phone.</p>
<p>That warning matters more than the setup. If you lose the password, the key or the passkey, your backup is permanently unusable. WhatsApp cannot open it for you, because it never holds the key. Write the password down on paper and keep it with your other important papers. A locked backup you cannot open is no backup at all.</p>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Lose the password or the 64-digit key and the encrypted backup is gone for good. WhatsApp cannot recover it.</div>
<h2>What a backup does not do</h2>
<p>No backup moves between phone types. An iCloud backup cannot restore on Android, and a Google backup cannot restore on an iPhone. The two clouds do not mix. If you switch from iPhone to Android, or the other way, use the direct transfer during setup instead, with a cable. The backup stays useful for the same phone, or a new phone of the same kind.</p>
<p>The copy does not last for ever, either. WhatsApp&#x27;s Help Center says Google may delete a backup that has not been updated in five months. That is why the schedule matters more than the first backup. A daily schedule keeps the copy fresh, and a forgotten backup quietly ages out.</p>
<p>And a backup is not a repair. It holds your chats, photos and videos. It does not hold your WhatsApp settings, your call history in full, or the media you never downloaded. A chat where the other person deleted messages stays deleted. Deleting the app leaves the cloud copy where it is. It stays in your Google Account or iCloud until you delete it there. What the backup does, it does well: it puts your conversations back on a new phone of the same kind, fast.</p>

<h2>When a backup fails, or you switch phones</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse4.mm.bing.net/th?q=person+restoring+a+new+phone+from+a+backup&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="person restoring a new phone from a backup" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Most failures come down to room or signal. If a backup will not finish, check the free space in your Google Account or iCloud first, then the space on the phone. A backup stuck at the start often means the phone is on mobile data with Back up using cellular off. Switch to Wi-Fi, plug the phone in, and try again. Large videos are the usual cause of a slow first backup.</p>
<p>To get chats back on a new phone of the same kind, install WhatsApp and sign in with the same number. Then tap Restore when it offers your backup. Use the same Google Account on Android, or the same iCloud account on iPhone. The restore runs before you can use the app, and it can take a while for a big backup. Do not skip it: once you pass the setup screen, the offer does not come back.</p>
<p>Switching between iPhone and Android needs the transfer tool, not a backup. On a new Android, the setup wizard moves your chats from the iPhone with a cable. On a new iPhone, the Move to iOS app does the same job from Android. Both need the phones close together and charged. And an exported chat gives you a text copy to keep, whatever phone you use next.</p>
<ul><li>Before the first backup: check the free room in your cloud account.</li><li>Include videos: clips can double the size of a backup.</li><li>Cellular off: let the first backup run on Wi-Fi.</li><li>Export chat: a text copy of one conversation.</li></ul>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Run a backup by hand the night before you switch phones. A fresh copy restores fastest.</div>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>How often should WhatsApp back up?</h3>
<p>Pick Daily if you chat every day. Chats change every day, and a daily backup means you lose at most a day if the phone goes. Weekly is fine if you barely use WhatsApp. Monthly is too slow for anyone who chats daily.</p>
<h3>Does WhatsApp back up my photos and videos too?</h3>
<p>Yes, if you let it. Photos back up by default, and videos need the Include videos switch on Android. Videos can double the size of a backup, so check your free cloud room first. Voice messages and documents back up with the chats.</p>
<h3>Can I read a WhatsApp backup without WhatsApp?</h3>
<p>No. The backup is a file only WhatsApp can open, and you cannot pull messages out of it by hand. If you need a readable copy of a chat, open that chat, tap the name at the top, then Export chat. It saves the messages as a text file, with media attached if you choose.</p>
<h3>What if I change my phone number?</h3>
<p>Change the number inside WhatsApp first, before you switch phones. Go to Settings, then Account, then Change number, and follow the steps. Your chats and your backup move with the number. If you change the number without doing this, the backup stays tied to the old one.</p>
<h3>Does backing up use my mobile data?</h3>
<p>Only if you let it. By default, WhatsApp backs up on Wi-Fi and waits when you are on mobile data. The Back up using cellular switch on Android lets it run on data, which can eat a plan fast on a big backup. Leave it off unless you have data to spare.</p>
