<h2>How to back up iPhone photos to a computer with a cable</h2>
<p>With a cable, you get the fastest copy of a full library. You plug the iPhone in, unlock it, and import. The photos land as real files on that computer. That is what makes it a backup.</p>
<p>Your computer decides which app does the work. A Mac uses the <strong>Photos app</strong>, which Apple builds into macOS. A Windows PC needs one extra install. iTunes from the Microsoft Store adds the driver Windows needs, and Microsoft Photos does the import. Both routes are free and need no iCloud.</p>
<p>Wireless routes suit smaller jobs. <strong>iCloud Photos</strong> copies your whole library without a cable, once it is turned on and your photos have finished uploading. AirDrop sends a few photos to a nearby Mac, and Image Capture saves plain files into a folder you pick. Pick the route that fits your computer and the job, then follow its steps below.</p>
<p>One setting can quietly shrink the copy. If iCloud Photos is on, check one setting first. With <strong>Optimize iPhone Storage</strong> on, some originals live only in iCloud, with smaller copies on the phone. A cable import can only copy what sits on the phone itself.</p>

<h2>Back up iPhone photos to a Mac with the Photos app</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=iphone+plugged+into+macbook+showing+photos&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="iphone plugged into macbook showing photos" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>The Photos app is the plain route on a Mac. It copies photos into your Mac photo library, which is the collection Photos manages for you. The import screen shows every photo and video on the phone.</p>
<p>Trust matters the first time you connect. The prompt on the iPhone reads Trust This Computer. Your iPhone asks whether to trust the computer, and it will not show its photos until you tap <strong>Trust</strong> and enter your passcode. That prompt protects your photos on a shared or borrowed computer. Tap Trust only on a computer you own or fully trust.</p>
<p>Use the Album menu to decide where the photos sit after the copy. Pick an album, make a new one, or leave it set to none. The tick box labelled <strong>Delete items</strong> removes photos from the phone after import, so leave it off for a backup.</p>
<p>Wait until the import bar clears before you pull the cable. Pulling it early can leave gaps. Check a few photos on the Mac after the copy. Open one full screen to check the date and the quality.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Connect your iPhone to your Mac with a USB cable.</li><li>Newer macOS versions may show an accessory dialog. Click Allow if it appears.</li><li>Launch the Photos app on your Mac.</li><li>Click your iPhone&#x27;s name in the Photos sidebar if the Import screen does not open.</li><li>Unlock your iPhone with your passcode and tap Trust if it asks.</li><li>Choose an album next to Album, or create a new one.</li><li>Select the photos you want and click Import Selected, or click Import All New Items.</li><li>Wait for the import to finish, then disconnect the cable.</li></ol>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Leave the Delete items box off. A backup means the photos stay on the phone too.</div>
<h2>Back up iPhone photos to a Windows PC</h2>
<p>Windows needs one extra install before it can see an iPhone. Get <strong>iTunes</strong> from the Microsoft Store. iTunes installs the Apple driver Windows needs to read the iPhone. Without that driver, the PC may not see the phone.</p>
<p>Your cable matters more than it looks. Some cheap cables carry power only, and they charge the phone while the PC sees nothing. Use the cable that came with the iPhone. If the phone charges but never appears in Photos, swap the cable first.</p>
<p>Apple warns about one iCloud trap on this route. If iCloud Photos is on, you must download the original, full-resolution versions of your photos to the iPhone before you import to a PC. Small preview copies on the phone will not give you full-quality files. Open <strong>Settings</strong>, tap your name, tap iCloud, tap Photos, and choose Download and Keep Originals. Check the phone has room first.</p>
<p>Format can trip up a Windows import too. iPhones can shoot in a space-saving format called HEIF, which some older Windows apps struggle to open. Apple says to open Settings, tap Camera, tap Formats, and choose <strong>Most Compatible</strong> for JPEG photos and H.264 video from now on. Older HEIF photos still copy across. If Windows cannot open a HEIF photo, get the free HEIF Image Extensions from the Microsoft Store.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Install iTunes from the Microsoft Store.</li><li>Connect your iPhone to the PC with a USB cable that carries data.</li><li>Unlock your iPhone with your passcode.</li><li>Tap Trust or Allow if the iPhone asks about this computer.</li><li>From the Start menu, open Photos on the PC.</li><li>Select Import, choose your iPhone, and follow the prompts.</li><li>Check File Explorer if Photos does not list the iPhone.</li><li>Pick the photos you want and the folder to save them in.</li></ol>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Trust only a computer you own. A trusted computer can read your photos, videos and contacts.</div>

<h2>Back up iPhone photos without a cable using iCloud</h2>
<p>iCloud Photos is Apple&#x27;s sync service for photos. When it is on, your whole library uploads to iCloud and appears on your Mac, your PC through iCloud for Windows, and on iCloud.com. You plug nothing in, and the first upload takes a while.</p>
<p>Turn it on from the iPhone first. Open <strong>Settings</strong>, tap your name at the top, tap iCloud, then tap Photos. Switch <strong>iCloud Photos</strong> on. <strong>Download and Keep Originals</strong> stores full files on the phone. Optimize iPhone Storage keeps small copies on the phone and full files in iCloud.</p>
<p>Your Mac picks the library up on its own. Sign in with the same Apple Account on the Mac, and your photos appear as they sync. On a Windows PC, install iCloud for Windows from the Microsoft Store, sign in with the same Apple Account, and turn on iCloud Photos. Apple says the synced photos land in your Pictures folder, inside a folder called <strong>iCloud Photos</strong>.</p>
<p>For a few photos, a browser works. On iCloud.com, sign in, open Photos, and download the shots you want. Remember how sync works here. A change on one device spreads to the rest.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Go to Settings on your iPhone and tap your name at the top.</li><li>Tap iCloud, then tap Photos.</li><li>Turn on iCloud Photos.</li><li>Choose Download and Keep Originals for full files on the phone.</li><li>On a Windows PC, install iCloud for Windows and sign in with the same Apple Account.</li><li>Turn on iCloud Photos and click Done.</li></ol>
<h2>Plain files in a folder: Image Capture and AirDrop</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th?q=iphone+photos+folder+open+on+mac+desktop&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="iphone photos folder open on mac desktop" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Some readers want plain files, not a managed library. <strong>Image Capture</strong> is a small app built into macOS, and it does exactly that. It copies photos as ordinary files into any folder you choose. That makes them easy to copy onto a drive or sort by hand.</p>
<p>In Image Capture on the Mac, click your iPhone under Devices in the sidebar. Pick a folder in the <strong>Import To</strong> menu. Select the photos you want, then click Download, or click Download All for the whole library. Files land in that folder as they copy. Nothing goes into the Photos app.</p>
<p>For a few photos at a time, AirDrop suits the job. Open Photos on the iPhone, select the shots, tap the share button, and tap <strong>AirDrop</strong>. Tap your Mac when it appears. The files land in the Downloads folder on the Mac. AirDrop moves photos over Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, so it slows down with hundreds of files.</p>
<p>Photos app imports sit inside the Mac library. Image Capture and Windows imports sit in folders you choose. iCloud copies sit in the cloud and on every synced device. For a real backup, keep one copy that does not sync back.</p>
<h2>Myths and mistakes that lose iPhone photos</h2>
<p>The most costly belief is that iCloud Photos is a backup. It is sync. Your library stays the same on every device signed in with the same Apple Account. Delete a photo on the phone and it leaves iCloud, your Mac and your PC too. That is why iCloud Photos alone is not a backup: a real backup stays put when the original changes.</p>
<p>Another belief says the import finished because the phone shows no error. Check the copy before you delete anything from the phone. Look in the folder or the Photos app on the computer and count the newest photos. Compare the file count and the latest dates with what the phone shows. If a photo is missing, run the import again before you free space on the phone.</p>
<p>Trusting a borrowed computer is a third mistake. The Trust prompt gives that computer access to your photos, videos and contacts. Use your own computer, and keep a second copy on a drive stored away from it.</p>
<p>Keep two copies. Copy the imported photos onto an external drive and store it somewhere safe. Open the drive every few months and look at a few photos. Treat it as a guess until you have looked at the files.</p>
<ul><li>Deleting from the phone: a synced photo deletes everywhere, including iCloud.</li><li>One copy only: a cable import is one copy. A drive failure still loses it.</li><li>Trusting a shared PC: a trusted computer can read photos and contacts.</li><li>Pulling the cable early: a cut import can leave gaps you spot too late.</li></ul>
<div class="note warn"><b>Common mistake:</b> Deleting a photo from your iPhone deletes it from iCloud and every synced device. Copy first, delete later.</div>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Do I need iCloud to back up iPhone photos to a computer?</h3>
<p>No. A cable and the Photos app copy photos without iCloud. On a Mac, open Photos and import. On a Windows PC, install iTunes first, then import with Photos. iCloud helps only when you want photos to sync without a cable.</p>
<h3>Why does my computer not see my iPhone?</h3>
<p>Check three things. Use a cable that carries data, not power only. Enter the passcode on the iPhone. Tap Trust when the phone asks about the computer. On a Windows PC, install iTunes first. Open Device Manager and check for Apple Mobile Device USB Driver if the phone still does not appear.</p>
<h3>Will deleting photos from my iPhone free space if I use iCloud Photos?</h3>
<p>It frees space on the phone, but it also deletes those photos from iCloud and every synced device. That is how sync works. Copy the photos to a computer or a drive first. Only then delete them from the phone.</p>
<h3>What format should my iPhone photos be in for a Windows PC?</h3>
<p>Choose Most Compatible in Settings, then Camera, then Formats. New photos save as JPEG and new videos as H.264. Older photos in the space-saving format still copy across.</p>
