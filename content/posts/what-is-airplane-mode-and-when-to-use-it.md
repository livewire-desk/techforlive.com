<h2>What is airplane mode and when to use it</h2>
<p>Airplane mode is a setting on every phone that cuts its wireless signals in <strong>one tap</strong>. The name comes from flying, because airlines ask passengers to stop their phones sending signals during a flight. The switch does far more than that, and most people use it on the ground too. You will find it in Control Center on iPhone and in Quick Settings on Android.</p>
<p>Your phone stays in touch through radios, small parts that send and receive signals. The cellular radio links your phone to the nearest mast for calls, texts and mobile data. The Wi-Fi radio links it to a router, and the Bluetooth radio links it to headphones, a watch or a car. Airplane mode tells these radios to stop, so your phone goes quiet on every network at once.</p>
<p>Put simply, one switch replaces several separate ones, so you never hunt through settings menus. Turn it on before take-off, and your phone stops searching for a signal while you fly. Turn it off after landing, and your phone reconnects to the network.</p>
<p>People reach for it for four reasons: flights, quiet time, a low battery and a stuck connection. This guide covers each one, and what still works while the switch is on. You do not need any app or extra setting, and nothing about the switch costs money.</p>

<h2>What airplane mode switches off</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse3.mm.bing.net/th?q=close-up+of+a+phone+quick+settings+panel+with+airplane+icon&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="close-up of a phone quick settings panel with airplane icon" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Cellular is the first thing airplane mode switches off. That means <strong>no regular calls, no text messages</strong> over the mobile network, and no mobile data. Your phone stops talking to the nearest mast, so nobody can reach you by phone number. Messages sent to you wait and arrive after you switch it off.</p>
<p>Wi-Fi and Bluetooth usually go off too. You can tap either one back on without leaving airplane mode, and some phones leave Bluetooth on. The makers picked different defaults, so check the two tiles after you tap the switch. They show the real state on your phone.</p>
<p>The phone also stops sending its own signal to find a network. That is also why the mode can help save battery while you travel. With cellular off, no app can use mobile data abroad, so roaming charges stop too.</p>
<p>Calling over Wi-Fi is the one exception worth knowing. If your carrier supports it and you switched it on, a normal phone-number call can still go over Wi-Fi while airplane mode is on. The call looks the same on your screen, and the number shown is your own. Turn Wi-Fi back on first, and the call goes through the wireless network instead of the mobile one.</p>

<h2>What still works with airplane mode on</h2>
<p>Nothing on the phone itself stops. Everything that lives on the phone keeps working, because none of it needs a signal. Your camera, clock, alarm, notes and calculator all run as normal. Music, films, books and games you downloaded before still play.</p>
<p>Both Wi-Fi and Bluetooth can come back while airplane mode stays on. If the airline allows it, you can still use Wi-Fi and Bluetooth in airplane mode. That lets you join the plane&#x27;s Wi-Fi and keep your headphones paired. Your phone remembers the choice, so Wi-Fi you turned back on may stay on next time. If you turned Bluetooth off in airplane mode, the phone keeps it off next time too.</p>
<p>Maps are a mixed case. A map you downloaded still opens and shows the streets you saved, but live traffic and searching for new places need a connection. GPS still works as well, since it only listens for satellite signals and sends nothing back. The same rule covers most apps: what you saved works, and what needs the internet waits. Photos, files and anything stored on the phone are all still there.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Download films, music and maps before you fly. Airplane mode never blocks what is already on your phone.</div>
<h2>How to turn airplane mode on and off</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse3.mm.bing.net/th?q=finger+tapping+the+airplane+mode+tile+on+a+phone+screen&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="finger tapping the airplane mode tile on a phone screen" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Most phones keep the switch one swipe away. On iPhone, open Control Center and tap the airplane button, or open Settings and use the Airplane Mode switch at the top. On a Pixel, swipe down twice to open Quick Settings and tap the airplane tile, or go to <code>Settings &gt; Network &amp; internet &gt; Airplane mode</code>. Other Android phones keep it in a similar place, usually under <code>Settings &gt; Network &amp; internet</code> or <code>Settings &gt; Connections</code>. A small airplane icon appears in the status bar, so you can see at a glance that it is on.</p>
<p>Turning it off is the same move in reverse. Tap the airplane button again, and your phone rejoins the mobile network and any Wi-Fi it remembers. Calls and texts that arrived while you were offline come through once the phone reconnects. Give it a few seconds, since the phone has to find the network again.</p>
<p>If your phone will not reconnect, toggle airplane mode on and off once. It forces the phone to search for a signal from scratch. The trick takes a moment, and it works on iPhone and Android alike. No setting changes, and nothing needs a restart.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Swipe down from the top of the screen to open Control Center on iPhone, or Quick Settings on Android.</li><li>Tap the airplane icon. It lights up, and a small airplane appears in the status bar.</li><li>To use Wi-Fi or Bluetooth, tap that button too while airplane mode stays on.</li><li>To switch everything back, tap the airplane icon again and wait for the signal bars to return.</li></ol>
<h2>When to use airplane mode on the ground</h2>
<p>Flights are the obvious use, but the switch earns its place the rest of the week too. It is the fastest way to silence your phone completely. Do Not Disturb can still let some calls through, while airplane mode stops them all. Use it for a meeting, a film, a nap or an evening without pings.</p>
<p>It also helps when your battery is low. A phone with no signal keeps hunting for one, and that hunt uses power. Airplane mode ends the hunt, which helps most where coverage is weak, in a basement or on a train through the countryside.</p>
<p>Travellers use it to dodge roaming charges. With cellular off, your phone cannot use mobile data abroad, so no background app can spend your allowance. Turn Wi-Fi back on for the hotel network, and you can message over Wi-Fi while the phone number stays silent.</p>
<p>Flaky connections are another good moment. If your phone shows bars but nothing loads, switch airplane mode on, wait a few seconds, and switch it off. The phone drops its connection and starts fresh, which clears many stuck connections.</p>
<ul><li>Before take-off: tap the switch, then turn Wi-Fi back on if the airline offers it.</li><li>Before bed: tap the switch when you want a night without calls.</li><li>Before a trip abroad: tap the switch, then use hotel Wi-Fi for messages.</li><li>When the signal drops: tap it on, count to five, tap it off.</li></ul>
<h2>Myths about airplane mode you can ignore</h2>
<p>One stubborn myth says airplane mode makes your phone charge <strong>twice as fast</strong>. It does not. Airplane mode does not change how fast your phone charges. Any effect on charging time is too small to notice.</p>
<p>Another says your phone is hidden or untraceable in airplane mode. It is not. The phone still holds your photos, files and past locations, and anyone holding it can read what is on it. Switching the radios off does not wipe anything, and it does not hide the phone from anyone holding it. Your photos, messages and browsing history stay on the phone exactly as they were. Airplane mode only stops new signals, and it is not a privacy tool.</p>
<p>A third says Wi-Fi never works in airplane mode. It does, when you turn it back on, and airlines that offer Wi-Fi expect you to. The rule on a plane is to keep cellular off and follow the crew. If they say switch everything off, do that. The crew may still ask you to switch Wi-Fi off as well on some flights, and their word is final.</p>
<p>Still, one more myth says airplane mode switches the phone off completely. It does not. The screen stays on, apps keep running, and the battery still drains, just more slowly. The only thing that stops is the radio chatter.</p>
<div class="note warn"><b>Common mistake:</b> Never turn cellular back on during a flight to check a message. The crew&#x27;s instruction stands, and one message can wait until you land.</div>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Does airplane mode turn off Wi-Fi?</h3>
<p>At first, yes, on most phones. You can turn Wi-Fi back on while airplane mode stays on, and your phone may remember the choice next time. On a flight, use it only if the airline allows Wi-Fi.</p>
<h3>Can I still get calls or texts in airplane mode?</h3>
<p>No. Callers usually get your voicemail or an unavailable tone, depending on your carrier. If Wi-Fi calling is turned on, a normal phone-number call can still go over Wi-Fi.</p>
<h3>Does airplane mode save battery?</h3>
<p>Yes, a little. With the radios quiet, the phone spends less energy staying in touch with the network. The gain is small, so it will not rescue a nearly dead battery.</p>
<h3>Should I use airplane mode instead of turning my phone off on a flight?</h3>
<p>Yes, that is what it is for. Airlines ask for airplane mode, not a powered-off phone, so you can still watch downloaded films and listen to music. Follow the crew if they ask for more.</p>
<h3>Why do my alarm and clock still work in airplane mode?</h3>
<p>Because alarms, timers and the clock run on the phone itself, so they carry on as normal. You can trust your morning alarm with the mode on.</p>
<h3>Can I call emergency services in airplane mode?</h3>
<p>Not over the mobile network, because the cellular radio is off. If Wi-Fi calling is turned on and your carrier supports it, an emergency call can go over Wi-Fi instead. Otherwise, switch airplane mode off first.</p>
