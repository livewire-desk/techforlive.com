<h2>What Signal changed and why your screenshots stopped</h2>
<p>Signal said in its blog post, reported by 9to5Mac and Engadget on 20 November, that it now restricts AI agents built into phone systems. The target is screen scanning. Assistants read what sits on your screen to answer questions. Private chats sit on that screen too.</p>
<p>Apple calls its version <strong>Onscreen Awareness</strong> with Siri AI, while Google&#x27;s Gemini assistant covers Android phones. Signal names both in its post. Captured chat text can leave your phone for processing elsewhere. That capture happens outside Signal&#x27;s control.</p>
<p>Windows got the same treatment last year. Screenshot blocking stopped Microsoft Recall from storing Signal windows by default. Phones now get the same treatment. The block covers screenshots and screen recordings inside the app.</p>
<p>The Hacker News reported the same change for Android and iOS. On iPhone the new setting is on by default. On Android the block reacts when an assistant tries to read the screen. Both phones show a message when capture fails.</p>
<p>Encryption was never the weak point in this fight. Messages travel sealed between phones. The screen shows them open, by design. Anything that reads screens reads open text. That simple fact drives the whole change.</p>

<h2>How to take screenshots in Signal again on iPhone</h2>
<p>Try a screenshot first and read the message Signal shows. Signal shows a message naming the screenshot-blocking setting when capture fails. That message confirms the new block caused the failure. A broken button shows no such message.</p>
<p>A prompt appears when capture is blocked. Engadget reported that the prompt offers to turn protection off. Turn it off only for the capture you need. Switch protection back on straight after.</p>
<p>Permanent off lives in Signal&#x27;s privacy settings, where the setting description says it prevents Siri AI from scanning Signal content on your phone. The same description warns it also blocks your screenshots and the App Switcher preview. One toggle controls both effects. 9to5Mac and Engadget both name the iPhone toggle Block Siri AI. Screen recordings fail the same way while the toggle stays on.</p>
<p>Leave the toggle on unless you truly need the capture. Each screenshot you take with the block off can be scanned the same way. Turn protection back on right after. Your contacts never agreed to the scan.</p>
<p>Turn protection back on as soon as the capture is done. Take the screenshot. Return to Privacy and switch the toggle on again. Nothing stays exposed overnight. Leaving it off should need a reason you can state out loud.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Open Signal and tap your profile picture to reach Settings.</li><li>Go to Privacy and find Block Siri AI.</li><li>Turn Block Siri AI off.</li><li>Take your screenshot, then return to Privacy and turn Block Siri AI back on.</li></ol>
<div class="note"><b>Check this:</b> Try a screenshot first. The failure message tells you the block is active.</div>
<h2>Android works differently: Screen security switches itself on</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="/media/hand-holding-an-android-phone-showing-the-settings-1767ad9370-800.webp" alt="Hand holding an Android phone showing the Settings menu" loading="lazy" decoding="async"><figcaption>Photo: Watford Media via Pexels, Pexels</figcaption></figure>
<p>Android uses Signal&#x27;s older <strong>Screen security</strong> setting, and Signal Support describes the path through your profile and Privacy on the phone. Some versions label the area differently. Follow the labels your screen shows.</p>
<p>The Android block is reactive, not always on, because Signal switches Screen security on when it detects an assistant reading the screen. A message explains what happened. Detection works only where Signal can spot the attempt.</p>
<p>You can switch Screen security on early instead. Early setup hides Signal from the recent-apps list as well. A blue privacy screen covers your chats in the app switcher. Screenshots inside Signal stop working while it stays on.</p>
<p>Turning Screen security off restores screenshots on Android. Decide per capture, not once for good.</p>
<p>Phone makers layer their own settings over Android. Menu names drift between brands. Search Settings for screen or capture if labels differ. The Signal toggle itself stays in Privacy. Start there every time.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Open Signal on Android and tap your profile picture.</li><li>Go to Privacy, then Screen security. Some versions label the area differently.</li><li>Turn Screen security on to block capture, or off to allow screenshots again.</li><li>Check the recent-apps list to confirm the blue privacy screen covers Signal.</li></ol>
<h2>What turning the block off exposes and what it cannot stop</h2>
<p>Screen capture is one route into your chats. Signal calls its own remedy incomplete. Content already stored on the device can be reached through the file system. Screenshot rules do not close that door.</p>
<p>Your setting covers your phone only, and Signal Support states this limit in plain words on its help page. A contact with protection off can capture your messages freely. Their assistant can scan the same chat on their screen. Group chats multiply that exposure.</p>
<p>Notifications sit outside the block as well, since previews can carry message text onto lock screens and into other apps. Signal wants system-level opt-outs for screens, files and notifications. No such standard exists yet, per its post.</p>
<p>Mac users get no screenshot block at all. Signal says macOS lacks the tools to block capture reliably. Its advice is to exclude Signal from Siri in system settings. That manual step replaces the missing block.</p>
<p>Group chats deserve extra care before any capture. Ten people wrote those lines, not one. Each writer trusted the chat to stay in the chat. Ask the group first. Silence is not consent here.</p>

<h2>Safer ways to save a chat without dropping the block</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse3.mm.bing.net/th?q=hand+writing+notes+beside+phone+on+desk&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="hand writing notes beside phone on desk" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Most screenshot needs have a calmer answer, such as copying the text you need and pasting it into your notes app. Ask the sender to resend a photo as a file. Both routes keep the block fully on.</p>
<p>Single captures still mean turning the block off, then on again. Take the screenshot. Switch protection back on right after. That beats leaving the toggle off and forgetting it. Forgotten toggles cause most exposure.</p>
<p>Apple lets users restrict which apps Siri can access, and Signal advises using that setting on Mac. On iPhone, the in-app block does the main work. Keep Block Siri AI on and the scanning route stays shut.</p>
<p>Check notification previews while you are in settings. Hide message text on the lock screen. Preview text is chat content too. Assistants and bystanders read previews with ease.</p>
<p>Test your lock screen after these changes. Lock the phone and wake it. Message text should stay hidden. Names alone reveal less than full lines. Small leaks add up over months.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>In Signal, open Privacy and confirm Block Siri AI stays on.</li><li>Hide lock-screen previews so message text stays off the lock screen.</li><li>Lock the phone and wake it to check what the lock screen shows.</li><li>Copy needed text into your notes instead of capturing the chat screen.</li></ol>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Need one capture? Turn the block off, take it, and switch it back on right away.</div>
<h2>Myths you can ignore about the screenshot block</h2>
<p>One myth says the block proves Signal chats are now screenshot-proof, yet your phone is only half of every conversation you have. The other screen answers to its owner&#x27;s settings. No app can control a camera pointed at a screen either.</p>
<p>Another myth blames a bug or a broken update, but the failure message names the setting on purpose in plain text. Signal chose this trade-off in public. Screenshot convenience lost to chat privacy. That choice is the story.</p>
<p>People also claim the block changes encryption. It does not. Messages travel with the same protection as before. The fight here is about capture after delivery. Delivered text on a screen is readable text.</p>
<p>Claims that turning the block off affects only you are wrong. Your contacts&#x27; words sit in the same chats. Scanning exposes every participant, per Signal&#x27;s post. That shared exposure is easy to forget in the moment.</p>
<p>Updates will move these labels again. Signal and the phone makers keep revising AI controls. Re-check Privacy after big system updates. Toggles sometimes reset to defaults. Five minutes twice a year keeps you current.</p>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Does the block stop the other person screenshotting?</h3>
<p>No. Signal Support says the block works on your own device only. The other person can still capture the chat on their phone. Treat every chat as copyable by its readers.</p>
<h3>Did Signal change how my messages are encrypted?</h3>
<p>Encryption stays the same. The change covers screen capture and on-device scanning only. Message encryption, contacts and chat history stay as they were. Your chats keep the same protection in transit.</p>
<h3>What about Signal on a Mac?</h3>
<p>Signal says it cannot block screenshots reliably on macOS. Apple lets users restrict which apps Siri can access. Signal advises using that setting on Mac to keep Siri away from Signal.</p>
<h3>I do not see the setting. What should I check?</h3>
<p>Update Signal, then check the Privacy settings again. On iPhone the control needs iOS 27. On Android the block can switch itself on after an assistant tries to read the screen.</p>
