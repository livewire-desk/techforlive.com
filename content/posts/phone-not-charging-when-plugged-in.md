<h2>Phone not charging when plugged in: read the first sign</h2>
<p>Plug the cable in and watch. Many phones buzz, chime or light up with a <strong>charging icon</strong> within a moment. That response tells you power reached the phone and the phone noticed it. Good news, in a way. The fault sits further along, in how much power arrives or how the battery accepts it.</p>
<p>Silence tells a different story. No buzz. No icon. The screen stays black. Power may be missing before it ever reaches the phone, or the battery may be too flat to respond. A fully drained phone can look dead on the charger at first, even with good gear. Silence alone does not prove a broken phone.</p>
<p>Keep the two cases apart in your head. Some phones say it out loud. Android may show Connected, not charging or Charging slowly on the lock screen. iPhones can show a charging paused message instead. Read that wording before you swap anything. It often names the branch for you. Nothing showing at all points at the outlet, the adapter, the cable or a blocked port. Work the branch that matches your phone, and finish it before you wander into the other one.</p>

<h2>Swap one part at a time from the wall inward</h2>
<p>Start at the wall. Sockets fail. Power strips fail too, and a strip switched off at the wall looks exactly like a dead phone. Plug a lamp into the same socket and see if it lights. No light means you found the fault already, and the phone was never the problem. Try a second socket before you blame any gear.</p>
<p>Move one link closer. Keep the same cable and swap the <strong>adapter</strong>, the small block that plugs into the wall. Then keep that adapter and swap the cable. Apple, Google and Samsung all give the same advice here: try a different cable or charger, and check each connection sits firmly at both ends. Read the small print on the adapter while you swap. The label lists its output, and a low-output brick can leave a busy modern phone flat even while plugged in. Match the adapter to what the phone maker lists for your model.</p>
<p>Your spare tablet settles arguments. Charge a tablet, earbuds case or second phone with the suspect cable and adapter. Spare charges happily? Your gear works, and suspicion moves to the phone itself. Spare sits silent too? The gear is guilty. Replace the failed part, keep the good parts, and test the phone again from the top.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Test the socket with a lamp, or move the adapter to a different wall socket.</li><li>Push the cable firmly into adapter and phone, then watch for the icon.</li><li>Swap the adapter for one you trust, keeping the same cable, and plug in again.</li><li>Swap the cable for one you trust, keeping the same adapter, and plug in again.</li><li>Charge a different device with the suspect cable and adapter.</li></ol>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Borrow before you buy. A neighbour&#x27;s cable for two minutes tells you more than a new cable bought blind.</div>
<h2>Lint in the port stops the plug seating fully</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=close+view+of+phone+charging+port&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="close view of phone charging port" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Pockets are filthy places. Lint, dust and crumbs ride into the <strong>charging port</strong> every day, and the plug presses them into a hard little pad at the back. The plug then stops a fraction short. It feels plugged in. It is not. Contact comes and goes with every nudge of the cable.</p>
<p>Turn the phone off first. Shine a torch into the port and look. Inspect the port with a flashlight and clear any debris before plugging in again. Loose fluff comes out with a <strong>dry wooden toothpick</strong> or a soft brush, worked gently along the edges. Lift debris out. Do not ram it deeper.</p>
<p>Metal is the enemy here. Pins, needles and paperclips can bend or snap the small contact tongue inside the port. Bent contacts turn a free clean into a paid repair. Keep liquids out as well. Work slowly and keep the phone face down so loose bits fall out, not in. A firm click and a flush seat mean the lint was your fault. Ports that still wobble or show bent, dark or missing contacts need a repair shop, not more poking.</p>
<ul><li>Phone off: switch the phone fully off before you clean the port.</li><li>Light first: use a torch so you can see what you are doing.</li><li>Wood or brush: a dry wooden toothpick or soft brush only, nothing metal.</li><li>Gentle lifts: ease loose debris out, never pack it further in.</li><li>Test the seat: the plug should sit flush without wiggling.</li></ul>

<h2>Restart and update clear stuck charging states</h2>
<p>Software can stick. Phones that charged fine yesterday may refuse today after an update, a crash or a flat battery. Restart it. On Samsung phones, hold the <strong>Side button</strong> and <strong>Volume down</strong> together, then tap Restart. Restart before you book anything. It clears stuck states that survive a cable swap. Restarting costs nothing and clears temporary system errors that block charging.</p>
<p>Frozen phones need the stronger version. Apple gives a <strong>force restart</strong> for iPhones that will not power on. Press volume up quickly, then volume down quickly. Hold the side button until the logo appears. Samsung phones restart on their own if you keep holding Side and Volume down. Pixel owners hold the Power button until the phone responds. Keys differ by model. Follow your maker&#x27;s steps for your phone.</p>
<p>Check the software version while you are there. Samsung points owners to <code>Settings &gt; Software update &gt; Check for updates</code>, and Apple says to make sure the latest iOS is installed. Charge settings matter too. Apple&#x27;s support pages note an iPhone may pause charging when the battery gets too warm. Newer iPhones can also hold at a set charge limit. On those iPhones the limit lives under <code>Settings &gt; Battery &gt; Charging</code>. Samsung phones hide a similar switch under <code>Settings &gt; Battery &gt; Battery protection</code>. Phones parked at the same percentage may be obeying a setting, not failing.</p>
<div class="note"><b>Check this:</b> Charging stuck at the same percentage every night? Look for a charge limit or battery protection setting before you blame the battery.</div>
<h2>A laptop or car socket may not feed the phone</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=phone+plugged+into+laptop+USB+port&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="phone plugged into laptop USB port" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Not every socket feeds a phone well. Laptop USB ports, car sockets and cheap hubs may supply far less power than a wall adapter. Sleeping laptops may cut their ports off entirely. Fast charging often will not work from a computer at all. Wall outlets charge faster than laptop ports. Slow from a laptop can be normal. Dead from a laptop proves little.</p>
<p>Judge the phone at the wall, idle, with the screen off and an adapter that suits it. Games and video calls can drain power as fast as a weak source supplies it. Leave the phone alone, then look at the percentage again. Rising while idle means charging works, whatever the laptop said.</p>
<p>Wireless charging splits the fault neatly. Try a wireless charger when wired charging fails. Charging on a pad but not by cable means the battery accepts charge. Suspicion falls on the cable or the port. Take the case off for the test, since a case can interfere with the plug or the pad. Centre the phone on the pad, too. A pad test with the phone sitting off-centre proves nothing. No charge either way points deeper, at the battery or the phone itself.</p>
<ul><li>Wall adapter: the fair test, with the phone idle and screen off.</li><li>Laptop USB: often slow, may stop when the laptop sleeps.</li><li>Car socket: varies widely, fine for topping up, poor for testing.</li><li>Wireless pad: centre the phone on the pad, case off, for a fair test.</li></ul>
<h2>Stop and get service when heat or damage shows</h2>
<p>Heat ends the home tests. Phones, cables or adapters that turn <strong>unusually hot</strong>, smell odd or show scorch marks come off the charger now. Phones may pause charging while they are too warm, then start again once cool. Move a warm phone and charger somewhere cooler. Warm is one thing. Too hot to hold comfortably is another. Unplug it and let it cool before any further test.</p>
<p>Look at the battery itself. Lifting back panels, bowing screens or cases that no longer close can mean a <strong>swollen battery</strong>. Do not press it flat, poke it or keep charging it. Keep it away from anything flammable and take it to the maker or a qualified repairer. Same rule for bent port contacts, split cable insulation or bent adapter prongs. Damaged accessories get replaced, never nursed along.</p>
<p>Know when the home checks are done. A phone that still will not charge after the restart, cable and outlet checks needs service. The same ending applies whatever badge sits on the back. Battery health screens can help a technician. On an iPhone, look under <code>Settings &gt; Battery &gt; Battery Health &amp; Charging</code>. One figure alone cannot separate a worn battery from a faulty port or board. Get the charging path inspected as a whole before paying for a battery swap.</p>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>The icon shows but the percentage keeps falling. What is wrong?</h3>
<p>The phone is spending power faster than the charger supplies it. Heavy apps, a weak source or a poor cable can all do this. Leave the phone alone on a charger you trust, screen off, and look again later. A percentage that still falls means the charging path or battery needs a proper inspection.</p>
<h3>My flat phone shows nothing on the charger. How long do I wait?</h3>
<p>A fully drained battery can look dead at first. Leave the phone on the charger for a while, then try a force restart and charge again. A long-drained phone may show no sign of life for a while before it responds. Wait, watch for heat, and only then move on to swapping parts.</p>
<h3>Charging only works when I hold the cable at an angle. Is the cable dead?</h3>
<p>Maybe. Worn cables can do it, and so can lint holding the plug short of full contact. Clean the port gently first, then try a different cable. Every cable needing the same angle trick points at a worn or damaged port.</p>
<h3>Can I clean the port with alcohol or a cotton bud?</h3>
<p>Skip both. Liquid can leave residue and corrosion inside the port, and cotton fibres pack down into the same lint pad you are trying to remove. Power the phone off and use a dry wooden toothpick or soft brush under a light instead. If debris will not shift, let a repairer clear it.</p>
<h3>The phone charges on a wireless pad but not by cable. What now?</h3>
<p>That result is useful. The battery accepts charge, so the fault likely sits in the cable or the wired port. Try a cable you trust, clean the port, and remove the case for the test. If wired charging still fails with good gear, have the port inspected.</p>
