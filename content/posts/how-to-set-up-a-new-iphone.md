<h2>How to set up a new iPhone: the order that works</h2>
<p>Setting up a new iPhone takes about half an hour of your attention, plus waiting time. The order matters more than the speed. Back up the old phone, charge both phones, then switch on the new one next to the old one. Quick Start does most of the work from there. You sign in, set up Face ID, pick how your data moves, then move your phone number.</p>
<p>Setup screens only appear on a fresh phone. Once you finish them, the transfer options go away. That catches people who rush past the data screen and then look for it later. You can still add accounts and apps by hand. You cannot run Quick Start again without erasing the phone and starting over. Slow down at the transfer screen and read each choice before you tap it.</p>
<p>This guide follows Apple&#x27;s setup pages. It covers the three starting points: an old iPhone, an Android phone, or no old phone at all. Each route ends at the same place: a new iPhone with your photos, messages, apps and number in place. With no old phone at all, set the phone up as new and sign in: anything stored in iCloud comes back once you sign in.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Back up the old phone to iCloud or a computer before you switch on the new one.</li><li>Turn on the new iPhone beside the old one and follow the prompts on both screens.</li></ol>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Do setup at home on your own Wi-Fi. A hotel or shop network can stall a transfer halfway, and a failed transfer means starting again.</div>

<h2>Get the old phone ready before you switch on the new one</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=hand+holding+old+iPhone+showing+software+update+screen&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="hand holding old iPhone showing software update screen" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>The smoothest moves start the night before, on the old phone. Update it first, because Quick Start works best when both phones run the latest software. Open Settings, tap General, then tap Software Update and install anything it offers. Then back the phone up. That backup is your safety net if the transfer stalls. Without one, a failed move can cost you photos.</p>
<p>Apple gives every iCloud account 5 GB of free storage, and a backup that will not fit simply will not run. Check the size in Settings, under your name, then iCloud, then iCloud Backup. If the backup is too big, back up to a computer with a cable instead. The cable route holds the whole phone.</p>
<p>Gather three things before you start. You need your Apple Account password, the one you use for the App Store and iCloud. You also need the old phone&#x27;s passcode and your Wi-Fi password, in case the new phone asks for it. Write them on paper if you are unsure. Forgetting a password during setup turns a quick job into an afternoon.</p>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Hold off erasing the old phone until the new one is checked. Erasing is the one step you cannot undo, and it wipes the only copy of anything that did not move.</div>
<h2>Move everything from your old iPhone with Quick Start</h2>
<p>Quick Start is Apple&#x27;s own way to copy one iPhone to another. It needs an old iPhone running iOS 11 or later, with Wi-Fi on and Bluetooth on. The old phone must be awake and showing its Home Screen. Turn on the new iPhone and hold it near the old one. A prompt appears on the old phone to set up the new one. Tap Continue and follow the screens. If the new phone asks to update its software during setup, let it install first, so the move runs on current software on both sides.</p>
<p>The setup then asks you to connect to Wi-Fi, and may ask you to activate your mobile service. You pick how your data travels: straight from the old phone, or downloaded from iCloud. Direct transfers tie up both phones until they end. An iCloud download lets you use the new phone at once while the rest arrives in the background.</p>
<p>Keep both phones close together and plugged into power until the move finishes. How long it takes depends on your network and how much data you have. Light phones may take minutes, and full ones can take much longer. Do not use either phone during a direct transfer.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>On the old iPhone, connect to Wi-Fi, switch on Bluetooth and open the Home Screen.</li><li>Turn on the new iPhone, hold it near the old one and follow the onscreen instructions.</li><li>Choose Transfer from iPhone or Download from iCloud, then wait for the move to finish.</li></ol>

<h2>Coming from Android: use Move to iOS</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th?q=Android+phone+and+iPhone+linked+by+a+USB-C+cable&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="Android phone and iPhone linked by a USB-C cable" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Android phones move to iPhone with Apple&#x27;s free Move to iOS app. It runs during setup, on the Transfer Your Apps and Data screen. Tap From Android there. On a phone that is already set up, that screen is gone. You would need to erase the phone and start again to use it. Plan the Android route before you switch the new phone on.</p>
<p>Get the free app from Google Play before you start. Turn on Wi-Fi on the Android phone and plug both phones into power. Check that everything you plan to move fits on the new iPhone, including files on a memory card. A USB-C cable between the two phones makes the move faster, Apple says, but it is not required.</p>
<p>Move to iOS copies contacts, message history, photos and videos, albums, files and calendars. It also moves call history, web bookmarks, mail accounts, accessibility and display settings, voice memos and WhatsApp chats. Music, books and PDFs do not move with it, Apple says, so shift those by hand. Your Android apps do not move either. The new iPhone suggests matching apps from the App Store, and you download them there.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>On the new iPhone, reach the Quick Start screen and tap Set Up Without Another Device.</li><li>Wait for the code to appear on the iPhone, then type it into the Android phone.</li><li>Leave both phones alone until the loading bar on the iPhone finishes, then tap Done on the Android phone.</li></ol>
<h2>Set up Face ID, your Apple Account and your phone number</h2>
<p>Face ID is the face scan that opens your iPhone and approves payments. It needs a passcode first, so set one during setup if you have not already. Hold the phone upright, keep your face inside the frame and turn your head slowly in a circle, twice. Face ID can wait. Add it later in Settings, under Face ID and Passcode, then Set Up Face ID.</p>
<p>An Apple Account is the sign-in that ties the phone to your purchases, photos and backups. It used to be called Apple ID. Sign in during setup when the screen asks. Signing in may send a verification code to a trusted device or number, so keep the old phone nearby to read it. If you have forgotten the password, use the forgot password link on that screen. Making a new account instead splits your purchases across two logins.</p>
<p>Your number moves separately from your data. iPhones sold in the US since iPhone 14 take only an eSIM. That is a digital SIM built into the phone, so there is no card to swap. Most models sold elsewhere still have a SIM tray, so you move the physical card across.</p>
<p>If your carrier supports eSIM Quick Transfer, the new iPhone offers to move your number during setup. Your carrier has to support the transfer. Both phones need to be signed in to the same Apple Account, with Bluetooth on, and both need iOS 16 or later. If your carrier does not support it, ask them for a QR code or a new eSIM. You can also move the physical SIM into the new phone. You can still move it after setup. Open Settings, tap Cellular, then Set Up Cellular or Add eSIM, and pick the plan to transfer. The old SIM switches off once the new one starts working. Send a text from the new phone before you wipe the old one.</p>
<h2>Finish the job and retire the old phone safely</h2>
<p>Give the new phone time to settle. Apps downloaded from iCloud arrive in the background, and their icons may look greyed out until they land. Open Photos, Messages, WhatsApp and your banking app, and check each one. Add your payment cards to Apple Pay again, as cards do not carry over to a new phone. That short check beats finding a gap next week.</p>
<p>Run a software update on the new phone, too. Open Settings, tap General, then Software Update, and install anything waiting there. Then check your storage in Settings, under General, then iPhone Storage. Full storage leaves no room for new photos, and the fix is deleting what you do not need.</p>
<p>Only now should the old phone go. Sign out of your Apple Account first, in Settings under your name, then Sign Out. That also switches off Find My, the lock that ties the phone to your account. Then erase it in Settings, under General, then Transfer or Reset iPhone, then Erase All Content and Settings. The phone restarts fresh, ready for its next owner.</p>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Can I set up a new iPhone without my old phone?</h3>
<p>Yes. Start fresh, or restore from an iCloud or computer backup if you made one. Sign in with your Apple Account and your photos, contacts and apps return from the backup.</p>
<h3>How long does it take to transfer data to a new iPhone?</h3>
<p>There is no set time. A direct move keeps both phones busy until it ends, so a full phone can take a while. An iCloud download lets you use the new phone while the rest arrives in the background.</p>
<h3>What if Quick Start does not appear on the old phone?</h3>
<p>Check that the old phone runs iOS 11 or later, has Wi-Fi and Bluetooth on, and is awake on the Home Screen. If the prompt has vanished, switch both phones off and on, then hold them close together again. Work or school phones enrolled in Apple School Manager or Apple Business cannot use Quick Start.</p>
<h3>Do I need my old phone after setup?</h3>
<p>Keep it until the new phone is checked properly. Photos, messages and WhatsApp chats are the things people find missing, so open each one before you erase anything. Once everything is confirmed, sign out of your Apple Account and erase the old phone.</p>
<h3>Can I move from Android after the iPhone is already set up?</h3>
<p>No. Move to iOS only runs during the first setup, on the Transfer Your Apps and Data screen. To use it, you have to start setup over from a fresh phone. Otherwise move your content by hand, through your Google account or a computer.</p>
