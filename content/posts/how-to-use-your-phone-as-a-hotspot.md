<h2>How to use your phone as a hotspot in plain terms</h2>
<p>A phone hotspot turns your phone into a small Wi-Fi router that shares its mobile signal with nearby devices. People also call this <strong>tethering</strong>. The other device sees a normal Wi-Fi name, joins it with a password, and sends traffic through your phone.</p>
<p>The name you pick on the other device is the <strong>hotspot name</strong>, which tech guides often call an SSID. That is just the label your phone broadcasts. A password protects that name, and anyone who knows both pieces can join and use your data. Keep both pieces private. Change the password if you shared it too widely.</p>
<p>Sharing passes your mobile signal to other devices that join the hotspot name you set. Apple notes <strong>Personal Hotspot</strong> does not forward an existing Wi-Fi link. You need mobile signal and enough data allowance for the session ahead. Carrier rules differ, so confirm hotspot use fits your current plan first. Plan ahead for travel days.</p>
<p>Keep the two devices close while you share because walls and distance weaken the link fast. A short gap on the same desk works best. You will notice the drop if you walk away.</p>

<h2>Turn on Personal Hotspot on an iPhone</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=iPhone+Personal+Hotspot+settings+screen&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="iPhone Personal Hotspot settings screen" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>iPhones call the feature <strong>Personal Hotspot</strong>, and you turn it on in Settings to show its name nearby. Other devices join it like any Wi-Fi network. They ask for the password you set, and once joined they use your mobile data for everything.</p>
<p>Setup takes one extra step at first because Apple says you need <strong>Cellular Data</strong> turned on. You then set up the hotspot once. After that, a single switch controls it, and the network name matches your iPhone name. Settings lets you change that name. The password sits on the same screen.</p>
<p>A phone can share its connection over Wi-Fi, over Bluetooth, or through a cable. Wi-Fi is the route most people pick for a laptop or tablet. Bluetooth pairs with one nearby device at a time. A cable ties a computer straight to the phone and tops up its battery during the session. Choose by the device in front of you.</p>
<p>Password changes happen on the same screen after setup, and you tap the password line to type a fresh mix. Network name changes need a new iPhone name, which you edit under Settings &gt; General &gt; About &gt; Name. Reconnect other devices after either change. That refresh clears old saved entries. Keep the new password private.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>One-time setup: go to Settings &gt; Cellular &gt; Set Up Personal Hotspot.</li><li>Open Settings &gt; Personal Hotspot on the iPhone.</li><li>Tap Allow Others to Join to turn sharing on.</li><li>Check the Wi-Fi password shown on that screen.</li><li>On the other device, open Wi-Fi settings.</li><li>Pick your iPhone name and enter the password.</li></ol>
<div class="note"><b>Check this:</b> Changed the password? Reconnect each device with the fresh mix.</div>
<h2>Turn on Wi-Fi hotspot on Android and Samsung</h2>
<p>Android phones use the name <strong>Wi-Fi hotspot</strong>, and Google puts a Hotspot tile in the swipe-down panel. Settings holds the same control as well. The other device then picks your hotspot name, enters the password you set, and shares your mobile data.</p>
<p>Samsung phones label it <strong>Mobile Hotspot</strong>, and Connections holds the switch for turning sharing on or off. Name and password live under Information. Older phones call that screen Configure. Screens vary by carrier and software version, but the idea stays the same across models you meet. You turn it on, then join from the other device.</p>
<p><strong>Band</strong> choice matters for older devices because that word means the radio range the hotspot uses. Samsung notes <code>2.4 GHz</code> works with more devices. The <code>5 GHz</code> option can give better speed nearby. Pick <code>2.4 GHz</code> if an older laptop cannot see the name, and switch back if speed matters more.</p>
<p>Quick joining works through a QR code on Samsung, and scanning replaces typing a long password. That helps when the password is long. Security options sit beside the password on many phones. Pick the strongest option your devices support. Keep the code private as well after sharing with guests.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Swipe down twice and tap the Hotspot tile.</li><li>Or open Settings &gt; Network and Internet &gt; Hotspot and tethering &gt; Wi-Fi hotspot.</li><li>On Samsung, open Settings &gt; Connections &gt; Mobile Hotspot and Tethering.</li><li>Turn on the hotspot switch and note the name and password.</li><li>Join that name from the other device with the password.</li></ol>
<h2>Wi-Fi, USB and Bluetooth tethering compared</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th?q=phone+plugged+into+laptop+with+cable&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="phone plugged into laptop with cable" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Wi-Fi sharing lets a small group join one mobile link at the same time. Google notes a Wi-Fi hotspot can serve <strong>several devices</strong> this way. Every connected device draws from that single connection. Speed drops as more devices pull data. Keep the group small for steady work.</p>
<p><strong>USB tethering</strong> links one computer by cable, and the phone charges while it shares data that way. That makes long sessions easier to manage. A data cable is required for this link. Some charge-only cables will not work. Some Macs need extra drivers for Android USB tethering on certain versions, so test the cable or use Wi-Fi.</p>
<p><strong>Bluetooth tethering</strong> pairs one device without a cable and uses less power than Wi-Fi sharing does. Speed is lower, so it suits light tasks. Email and web pages cope well. Large downloads feel slow on this link. Pick it when battery matters more than speed and the task stays light from start to finish.</p>
<p>Your choice depends on the job in front of you and the devices you need to connect today. Use Wi-Fi for a laptop and tablet together. Use USB for one computer all afternoon. Use Bluetooth for a quick check on the train. Each route draws from the same data allowance, and none of them creates free data for you. Turn the hotspot off when you finish. That saves battery and stops stray joins.</p>

<h2>Watch data, battery and safety while sharing</h2>
<p>Every byte through the hotspot counts because it comes from your <strong>mobile data allowance</strong> on that plan. A laptop cannot tell the difference. It sees normal <code>Wi-Fi</code> and acts that way, so big updates can start without any warning. Cloud backups can run the same way.</p>
<p>Pause heavy jobs before you connect and check the laptop update settings first for safety. Turn off auto backup for the session. Watch the data counter on your phone. This habit prevents a nasty surprise later and helps you stay in control of the session. Short sessions keep costs predictable.</p>
<p>Battery drain rises while sharing because the phone runs two radios at once for the link. It also works harder to pass traffic. Plug the phone in for long sessions, and a <strong>power bank</strong> helps on the move away from home. Heat is normal, but keep vents clear.</p>
<p>Set a <strong>strong password</strong> before you share and use a mix you can type without errors. Avoid names, dates and simple patterns. Turn the hotspot off when done to stop neighbours joining by mistake and end the drain. Change the password after lending access. Treat it like a house key. You stay in charge of who connects. Small habits prevent big bills.</p>
<ul><li>Data check: confirm your allowance covers the session.</li><li>Password set: use a strong mix you can type.</li><li>Power plan: plug in or carry a power bank.</li></ul>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> A laptop on your hotspot can run big updates. Pause them before you connect.</div>
<h2>Fix common hotspot problems without guesswork</h2>
<p>Start with the simplest checks and confirm <strong>mobile data</strong> works on the phone itself first. Open a web page without Wi-Fi. If that fails, the hotspot will fail too, so move to a spot with stronger signal. Try the hotspot again after that move.</p>
<p>Check the name and password next, and type the password again with care for symbols. Watch for capital letters in the mix. Forget the network on the other device. Join it fresh with the correct password, and that clears a bad saved entry for good. Slow typing prevents most errors.</p>
<p>Missing name points to the <strong>band</strong> because older laptops may miss <code>5 GHz</code> networks nearby. Switch the hotspot to <code>2.4 GHz</code>. Samsung describes that trade-off in its guide. Keep devices close while you test because walls can hide a weak signal from view. Move closer and test again.</p>
<p>Hidden switch often means a settings search helps. Use the search box at the top of Settings. Type hotspot and open the matching result. Restart both devices if settings look right, and a fresh start clears many stuck links. Test with one device first. Add others only after that link holds. Patience beats random tapping here. Stay calm.</p>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Does USB tethering charge my phone?</h3>
<p>Yes, on most phones the cable charges while sharing. Long sessions feel easier that way. Use a data cable for the link. Keep the phone cool during long use.</p>
<h3>Why does my battery drop so fast?</h3>
<p>Sharing keeps two radios busy at once. The phone also passes traffic for other devices. Plug it in for long sessions. Turn sharing off when you finish.</p>
<h3>How many devices can join at once?</h3>
<p>Several devices can join by Wi-Fi. Google notes the Wi-Fi hotspot supports several at once, but speed falls as more devices pull data. Keep the group small for steady work.</p>
<h3>What if the hotspot option is missing?</h3>
<p>Open Settings and use the search box. Type hotspot and tap the matching result. Restart the phone if the result stays hidden. Try again after the restart finishes.</p>
