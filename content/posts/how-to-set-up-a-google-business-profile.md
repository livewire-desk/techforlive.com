<h2>How to set up a Google Business Profile: the short version</h2>
<p>A Google Business Profile is the free listing that puts a business on Google Search and Google Maps, with its address, hours, phone number, photos and reviews in one place. Setting one up costs nothing, and the form itself takes well under an hour. Verification is the part that takes time, because Google has to be sure the business is real and that you run it.</p>
<p>The process has four parts. First, check that the business qualifies and that no listing already exists. Then add or claim the profile with its real name and details. Then verify it with whichever method Google offers you. Fill in the listing properly, with photos, services and hours, so customers can act on what they find.</p>
<p>One warning up front: you do not choose the verification method. Google picks it from the type of business, the information it already holds, your region and your hours, and the choice cannot be changed. Google may request a verification video for some business types; availability depends on the account and region. If you plan for that, the rest of the setup is straightforward.</p>

<h2>Check you qualify, and look for an existing listing</h2>
<p>Google&#x27;s test for a profile is short: the business must make in-person contact with customers during its stated hours. A shop, a café, a plumber who visits homes, and a clinic all qualify. An online-only business, a lead-generation site, a rental property, and anything using a PO box or a mailbox in a remote location do not. If the business never meets a customer face to face, a profile is not the right tool.</p>
<p>Before you create anything, search Google Maps for the business name and town. Google may already hold a listing, made from public records or a customer suggestion. If one exists, claim it instead of starting a second one: two listings for the same business confuse customers and split reviews. On the listing, choose the option to claim the business, and Google will take you through ownership checks.</p>
<p>Use a Google Account that belongs to the business and that you will always control, ideally one tied to the business email address. If you manage profiles for clients, create each one under the client&#x27;s own account, not yours. Losing access to the account means losing control of the listing, and recovering it later takes far longer than setting it up properly now.</p>
<h2>Create the profile, step by step</h2>
<p>Start at business.google.com/add and sign in. Type the business name exactly as customers know it, with no extra keywords, city names or marketing phrases added. Google will show any matching profiles before it lets you create a new one, which is your last chance to claim an existing listing instead.</p>
<p>Next, pick the category that fits best, such as plumber, bakery or dentist. The category decides which searches the listing can appear for, so choose the closest real match rather than the broadest one. Then add the address, or a service area if customers come to you or you visit them. A service-area business still gives Google an address for verification, but the address stays hidden from the public.</p>
<p>Add a phone number you control, a number where customers can actually reach the business, and the website if there is one. Then set the customer-facing hours. If the business only works by appointment, Google says not to list hours at all. Check every detail before moving on, because changing the name, address or category later can trigger another round of checks.</p>

<h2>Verify the business: the method Google picks</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=person+filming+a+short+video+of+their+shop+front+on+a+phone&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="person filming a short video of their shop front on a phone" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>A new profile does not show properly until it is verified, and Google decides how that happens. Google selects a verification method based on the business type and region; methods include phone, email, postcard, or video. The available method and the time until the profile becomes public vary by account and location. Which method you are offered, and how long it takes, also varies by country and business category.</p>
<p>If Google requests a video, follow the on-screen instructions in the verification flow; they require a live recording demonstrating the business location and operations. Follow Google&#x27;s guidance shown at the time of verification. Film the street and the sign outside, the inside with tools or stock, and something that ties you to the business.</p>
<p>The other methods are simpler when they appear. Phone or SMS codes are typically delivered quickly, subject to network and account conditions. Postcards include a verification code and can take up to several weeks to arrive; the flow shows the expiration window for that code. Instant verification is offered when the same website is already verified in Search Console. Once you finish your part, Google reviews the submission, and the time it takes varies by method.</p>
<h2>Fill in the profile so customers can use it</h2>
<p>Verification only proves the business is real. The listing still needs filling in before it helps anyone. Add photos of the outside, the inside and the work, because customers decide from pictures before they read anything. Add the services or products with plain descriptions, and keep the hours current, including holiday changes. A listing with wrong hours sends people to a closed door.</p>
<p>Use the posts feature for news that matters: a new service, changed hours, or a seasonal offer. Keep posts short and factual, and remove them when they expire. Add a description of the business in plain words, saying what it does and who it serves. Avoid stuffing the description with keywords or city names, because Google can penalise that and customers read it as spam.</p>
<p>Reviews are worth asking for, because they count towards how prominently Google shows the listing in local searches. Ask customers after a good job, and make it easy with a short link from the profile dashboard. Never offer anything in return for a review, and never pay for one: Google forbids incentives, and fake reviews are against the law in some countries. Answer every review, good or bad, in a sentence or two.</p>
<h2>Mistakes that delay or damage a listing</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse2.mm.bing.net/th?q=person+reading+a+rejection+email+on+a+laptop&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="person reading a rejection email on a laptop" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>The most common delay is changing details while verification is running. If Google sends a postcard, do not change the name, address or category while you wait, and do not ask for a second code, because that cancels the first. One change can restart the whole process, so sit still until the code arrives and the review is done. If a postcard never arrives, wait for the full delivery window, then use the help option in the profile dashboard to ask Google what to do next.</p>
<p>The most common damage is a duplicate listing. If the profile is rejected or the review drags on, do not create a second one to speed things up. Two listings split reviews and confuse Maps, and merging them later is slow. Fix the one thing the rejection email names, then try again with the same listing. The email says why it was rejected, so read it before acting.</p>
<p>Watch for scams. Google never asks for payment information over the phone, and it never guarantees a special spot in its products for a fee. Anyone who calls offering a top ranking for money is not from Google. If a call asks for card details, hang up.</p>

<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Is a Google Business Profile really free?</h3>
<p>Yes. Google does not charge to create, verify or run a profile, and there is no paid version that ranks higher. Anyone asking for payment to set one up is selling a service, not a Google fee.</p>
<h3>How long does verification take?</h3>
<p>It depends on the method Google offers. A phone or email code is usually quick, a video review takes longer, and a postcard can take up to several weeks. The profile is not fully public until verification finishes.</p>
<h3>What if my business has no street address?</h3>
<p>Choose the service-area option and list the places you serve. You still give Google an address for verification, but it stays hidden from the public listing.</p>
<h3>Can I change the business name later?</h3>
<p>Yes, but do it carefully. Changing the name, address or category can trigger another verification, so make changes one at a time and only when the details are wrong.</p>
<h3>What if someone else owns my listing?</h3>
<p>Use the &#x27;Request access&#x27; option on the profile. Google emails the current owner, who has seven days to respond. If there is no response, Google reviews your claim.</p>
