<h2>Two histories, two places to clear them</h2>
<p>Most people mean one thing when they say search history. Google keeps <strong>two separate records</strong>, though, and clearing one does not clear the other. One record sits in your <strong>Google Account</strong> and follows you when you sign in. Its partner sits in Chrome on the phone or computer in front of you. Keep the two lists apart in your head.</p>
<p>The account record has a name: <code>Web &amp; App Activity</code>. <strong>My Activity</strong> lists the searches and other activity Google saves to your account while <code>Web &amp; App Activity</code> stays switched on. Erasing happens on that same page. That page holds the whole account list. Nothing deleted there touches the list stored inside Chrome itself.</p>
<p>Chrome history stays local. Simpler, too. It is the list of pages and searches kept by the browser on that one device. Delete it and you clear that device&#x27;s list. Sign in to Chrome with sync on, though, and deleting synced data can remove it from your other devices and your account too. This detail matters, and the Chrome section below covers it in full. Local lists never travel on their own.</p>

<h2>How to delete google search history in My Activity</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse1.mm.bing.net/th?q=Google+My+Activity+delete+activity+screen&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="Google My Activity delete activity screen" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Open <strong>My Activity</strong> while signed in to the Google Account you want to clean. Saved activity for that account appears there, newest items near the top. Look for the Delete option on the page. Menus can look slightly different as Google updates its settings pages, so hunt for the same words rather than the same layout. Pause before you tap. Filters on the page narrow that list by date or product.</p>
<p>Choose the time range before you confirm anything. <strong>All time</strong> wipes the whole saved history for that account. A shorter range clears only recent activity and leaves older searches in place. Confirming is final, with no undo offered. Once erased, saved activity <strong>cannot be recovered</strong>, so read the range on screen twice. The range on screen is what counts.</p>
<p>Deleting starts the removal of that data from Google&#x27;s product and storage systems. Some data may be kept for a limited time for legal or business reasons, Google says. That is normal wording for a big storage system. For your own use, treat the delete as done once My Activity no longer lists those searches, then check the page again.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Sign in to the right Google Account in My Activity.</li><li>Tap Delete on the My Activity page.</li><li>Select All time to clear the whole saved history.</li><li>Review the choice on the next screen.</li><li>Confirm with Delete to erase it for good.</li></ol>
<div class="note warn"><b>Warning:</b> Once you confirm a delete in My Activity, that history cannot be recovered. Check the date range before you tap Delete.</div>
<h2>Delete one search, one day, or one product only</h2>
<p>A full wipe is not always what you want. One search on a shared computer, or one afternoon, may need to go, and My Activity lets you erase <strong>single items</strong> instead of everything. Find the item in the list, open its options, and choose <strong>Delete</strong> for that item alone. Everything else in your history stays exactly where it was. Small deletes suit shared devices best.</p>
<p>Filters make that job faster when the list is long. Filtering by <strong>date</strong> narrows the list to one day or one stretch of days. Product filters narrow it to activity from one Google service. Combine a date filter with a careful read, and you can clear one topic without touching the rest. Narrow lists are easier to check. Small lists hide nothing.</p>
<p>Take your time on a shared account or a family tablet. Items can look alike, and the delete confirm does not offer an undo. Work down the filtered list one item at a time. Stop when the page shows only activity you are happy to keep. A careful pass now avoids a second cleanup later.</p>
<ul><li>Single item: erase one search and leave everything else in place.</li><li>Date range: clear one day or one stretch of days only.</li><li>Product filter: limit the list to activity from one Google service.</li></ul>
<h2>Set your history to delete itself on a timer</h2>
<p>Manual deleting works once, then the list starts growing again. Timers keep working after you close the page. Google offers an auto-delete setting for <code>Web &amp; App Activity</code>, and it clears activity older than the period you pick. Three choices exist: <strong>3 months, 18 months or 36 months</strong>. You can also pick the option that turns auto-delete off. Set it once. Done.</p>
<p>Find the setting in My Activity under Controls, then <code>Web &amp; App Activity</code>, then <strong>Auto-delete</strong>. Another route runs through your Google Account: open Data &amp; privacy, find the history settings, and open <code>Web &amp; App Activity</code> there. Either route lands on the same choice. Pick a period, save it, and older activity clears itself from then on.</p>
<p>Stopping new saves is a different switch entirely. In the same Controls area, choose <strong>Turn off</strong> for <code>Web &amp; App Activity</code> to stop Google saving new activity to your account. An option also exists that turns the setting off and deletes saved activity at the same time. Read that screen slowly, because the two choices do different jobs. Just one tap apart.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Start in My Activity and choose Controls.</li><li>Go to Web &amp; App Activity, then Auto-delete.</li><li>Choose 3 months, 18 months or 36 months.</li><li>Save the choice to start the timer.</li></ol>
<div class="note"><b>Tip:</b> Auto-delete only clears activity older than the period you pick. It does not stop new searches from being saved today.</div>

<h2>Clear Chrome history on this phone or computer</h2>
<figure class="fig"><img src="https://tse3.mm.bing.net/th?q=Chrome+delete+browsing+data+dialog&amp;w=560&amp;h=380&amp;c=7&amp;rs=1&amp;p=0&amp;o=5&amp;pid=1.7" alt="Chrome delete browsing data dialog" loading="lazy" decoding="async"></figure>
<p>Inside Chrome, the list is separate from your account history. Launch Chrome and choose the More menu, the three dots in the corner. Select <strong>Delete browsing data</strong> from that menu. On a phone, open the Chrome app menu, go to History, then choose the clear browsing data option. Wording varies a little between versions, so look for the same idea. Menus hide in plain sight sometimes.</p>
<p>Next, choose a time range, from the last hour up to <strong>All time</strong>. Tick <strong>Browsing history</strong> so the list itself gets cleared. Other boxes, like cookies, clear other stored data. Cookies, the small files sites save in your browser, can sign you out when cleared, so leave them unticked unless you want that too.</p>
<p>Signed in to Chrome with sync on? Deleting synced browsing data removes it from your other devices and from your account as well, per Chrome Help. Signed out, the delete only affects this one device. Hence a search can vanish from your laptop yet still sit on your phone. Each device needs its own check. Repeat it everywhere. Phones hide lists well.</p>
<ol class="steps"><li>Launch Chrome and tap the three-dot More menu.</li><li>Tap Delete browsing data from the list.</li><li>Set a time range, such as All time.</li><li>Tick Browsing history, then choose Delete data.</li></ol>
<h2>Common mistakes that leave searches behind</h2>
<p>One list can look clean while the other still holds everything. Emptying Chrome on your phone leaves <strong>My Activity</strong> in your account untouched. Wiping your account does not empty <strong>Chrome</strong> on a tablet at home. Each list needs its own delete, in its own place, on its own screen. Only one of the two lists got cleared. Habit beats hope here.</p>
<p>Incognito mode helps before you search, not after. Searches made in Incognito are <strong>not saved</strong> to your Google Account, per Google&#x27;s own tip. Incognito suits shared computers for exactly that reason. It does nothing about searches you already made in a normal window, though, and those old searches still need a manual delete from you. Private windows never clean up old searches.</p>
<p>Signed-out searching can still leave a trail on the device. Using the Google app while signed out can save history on that computer or phone instead. So check the device list as well as the account list. Checking both places is the only way to be sure. Shared devices deserve both checks, every time. Two minutes covers both lists. Make it monthly.</p>
<ul><li>Account list: open My Activity and confirm old searches are gone.</li><li>Device list: open Chrome history on each phone and computer.</li><li>Timer set: check auto-delete holds the period you picked.</li><li>Saving switch: confirm Web &amp; App Activity matches your choice.</li></ul>
<h2>Quick answers</h2>
<h3>Does deleting history stop Google saving new searches?</h3>
<p>No. Erasing old activity clears what is already saved, and new searches keep saving while Web &amp; App Activity stays on. Open Controls in My Activity and turn that setting off to stop new saves. Auto-delete sits between the two: it keeps saving, then clears older activity on a timer.</p>
<h3>Can I get deleted searches back later?</h3>
<p>Recovery is not possible after that point. Erased activity cannot be recovered once you confirm the delete, per Google. So the time range on screen deserves a second read. Start with a short range if you feel unsure.</p>
<h3>Why do old searches still appear on my phone?</h3>
<p>Your phone likely holds its own Chrome history, separate from your account list. Clear browsing data in the Chrome app on that phone too. Signed-out use can also save history on the device itself, so check there as well.</p>
<h3>What does Incognito mode actually stop?</h3>
<p>While you use it, Incognito stops searches from being saved to your Google Account. Searches from normal windows stay put, and saved history stays saved. Use it before a search on a shared device, not as a cleanup tool after.</p>
